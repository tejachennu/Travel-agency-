A Dubai trip from Ahmedabad is the classic first international holiday for many families in Gujarat, and for good reason. The non-stop flight takes about three hours, the city is safe and spotless, vegetarian food is everywhere, and you can fit desert dunes, the world's tallest building and a beach into one long weekend. Our travellers keep telling us the same thing: *"perfectly balanced — iconic landmarks, but time to relax too."*

> **The short version:** Most Indians need a **UAE tourist e-visa** approved before flying, though holders of valid **US or EU visas/residence** (and a few others) can get a **14-day visa on arrival**. Fly non-stop from **Ahmedabad (AMD) to Dubai (DXB)** in about 3 hours, go between **November and March**, and plan **4 nights and 5 days** for the highlights.

## Dubai visa for Indians (2026)

### Tourist e-visa (most travellers)

**Most Indian passport holders need a UAE tourist visa approved before they fly.** It's an e-visa, with no embassy visit, applied for through a UAE-approved channel such as an airline or travel agency. Common options are **30-day and 60-day** tourist visas, single or multiple entry. Approval usually takes a few working days, but apply at least two weeks ahead in peak season.

Typical documents:

- passport valid for at least **six months**, with a clear colour scan of the front and back pages,
- a recent photo on a white background,
- confirmed **return ticket**,
- **hotel booking** (or the address of relatives you're staying with),
- PAN card, for some applications.

Airlines and UAE immigration have also been checking that visitors can support themselves. Reports have mentioned roughly **AED 3,000 (or equivalent) in cash or on a card**. Carry a forex card or credit card and your bookings on paper.

### Visa on arrival: who qualifies

Some Indians can skip the e-visa and get a **14-day visa on arrival**, extendable once, if they hold an **ordinary passport valid for six months** plus one of these:

- a valid **US visa**, **US green card** or US residence,
- a valid **EU (Schengen) visa** or **EU residence permit**,
- a residence permit or visa from **Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand or Canada** (added in February 2025).

**UK visa holders, take note:** reports in mid-2026 said the UAE removed UK visas and residence permits from this list for Indian nationals. If a UK visa is your only qualifying document, apply for a normal e-visa. Rules here change often, so confirm with the ICP website or your airline before flying on a visa-on-arrival plan.

### Overstay and passport tips

Leave on time. UAE overstay fines are charged per day. Check that your passport has blank pages and isn't damaged, as airlines can refuse boarding for torn or water-damaged passports.

## Flights from Ahmedabad to Dubai

Ahmedabad's **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport (AMD)** has non-stop flights to **Dubai International (DXB)** on **Emirates, flydubai, IndiGo and SpiceJet**, with roughly 25 to 35 departures a week depending on the season. The flight takes about **3 hours** (westbound) and the UAE is **1 hour 30 minutes behind IST**.

Tips for Gujarat travellers:

- **Diwali, Christmas and the May–June school holidays** are the busiest weeks, so book two to three months ahead.
- **Mid-week departures** (Tuesday to Thursday) are usually cheaper.
- Low-cost tickets may not include checked baggage. Add it before leaving room for the shopping.
- From DXB, Downtown and Bur Dubai are about **20 to 30 minutes** by road. The **Dubai Metro Red Line** connects Terminals 1 and 3.

## Best time to visit Dubai

| Months | Weather | What's on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| November to March | Pleasant days, cool evenings | Peak season; Global Village, Miracle Garden and desert camps in full swing; Dubai Shopping Festival (usually December–January) |
| October, April | Warm to hot | Seasonal attractions open, good hotel deals |
| May | Hot | Seasonal parks close around the end of the month |
| June to September | Very hot and humid | Indoor attractions, Dubai Summer Surprises sales, lowest hotel rates |

For the 2026–27 season, **Dubai Miracle Garden** reopened on **8 October 2026** (running to around end-May 2027). **Global Village** Season 31 opens on **14 October 2026**, with evening opening hours. Dubai is much less fun from June to August with kids, because afternoons outside are uncomfortable.

## A 4N/5D Dubai itinerary that works for families

| Day | Plan | Pace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Arrive, check in, Marina dhow cruise with dinner | Easy |
| 2 | City tour, Dubai Frame, Palm, Burj Khalifa at sunset, Dubai Fountain | Full |
| 3 | Slow morning, afternoon desert safari with BBQ dinner | Moderate |
| 4 | Abu Dhabi day trip, or Miracle Garden and Global Village | Full |
| 5 | Gold Souk or a mall, airport drop | Easy |

### Day 1: Arrive and dhow cruise
Airport pick-up and hotel check-in. In the evening, take a **dhow cruise with dinner** along Dubai Marina or Dubai Creek. It's an easy first night after an early flight.

### Day 2: City tour and Burj Khalifa
Start with a photo stop at Jumeirah Mosque, then the **Dubai Frame**, a drive along the **Palm Jumeirah** past Atlantis, and **Burj Khalifa "At the Top"** timed for sunset. Prime slots sell out, so book ahead. Finish with the free **Dubai Fountain** show outside Dubai Mall.

### Day 3: Desert safari
Desert camps are about **45 to 60 minutes** outside the city. Expect **dune bashing, camel rides, sandboarding, henna** and a **BBQ dinner** with live shows. Ask for vegetarian or Jain meals when booking. Pregnant travellers, seniors with back problems and very young children can skip dune bashing and join at the camp.

### Day 4: Abu Dhabi, or Miracle Garden and Global Village
Abu Dhabi is about **1.5 hours** by road. Most Gujarati families include the **BAPS Hindu Mandir** at Abu Mureikha, on the Dubai side of Abu Dhabi. Visitors must **register online in advance** at the mandir's official site, and it's **closed on Mondays**.

Then visit the **Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque**, which introduced a small entry fee for tourists in September 2026. Book on its official website, and wear modest, full-length clothing; women need to cover their hair.

Prefer to stay in Dubai? Spend the late afternoon at **Miracle Garden** and the evening at **Global Village**.

### Day 5: Shopping and fly home
Visit the **Gold Souk** and spice souk in Deira (take an abra across the Creek), or Dubai Mall or Mall of the Emirates, before your airport drop.

Use it for a **theme park day** (IMG Worlds, Dubai Parks and Resorts, or Ferrari World and Yas Waterworld in Abu Dhabi), **Museum of the Future**, or a lazy day on **JBR or Kite Beach**.

Choosing a hotel **within walking distance of a metro station** saves a lot on taxis.

## Vegetarian and Jain food

Dubai is one of the easiest international destinations for Gujarati food habits. **Bur Dubai (around Meena Bazaar)** and **Karama** have Gujarati thali, Rajasthani, Udupi and Punjabi vegetarian restaurants. Many will cook **Jain food without onion, garlic or root vegetables** on request. Big malls have Indian chains in the food courts.

Desert camps and dinner cruises serve vegetarian buffets. Request Jain meals in advance, because they're cooked separately.

## What a Dubai trip costs: the main drivers

We won't quote a generic number, because these factors swing the total widely:

- **Flight dates:** Diwali and Christmas fares can be several times off-season fares.
- **Hotel area and category:** Bur Dubai vs Downtown vs Palm.
- **Visa type:** 30-day vs 60-day, single vs multiple entry.
- **Attraction tickets:** Burj Khalifa prime-time slots, theme parks and Museum of the Future add up. Combo passes usually beat single tickets.
- **Private vs shared transfers** and the Abu Dhabi day trip.

Dubai hotels also add a nightly **Tourism Dirham fee** per room, usually paid at check-out.

## Packing and practical tips

- **Clothing:** light cottons in winter plus a jacket for desert evenings and air-conditioned malls. Pack modest outfits for mosques and the mandir.
- **Money:** a forex card plus some dirhams for souks and tips. Cards work almost everywhere.
- **Medicines:** carry prescriptions for any medicine you bring. The UAE strictly controls some common painkillers and sleeping pills.
- **Phone:** an international roaming pack or local eSIM. Most Indian apps work normally.
- **Public behaviour:** no public displays of affection, no photos of people without permission, and no drinking in public places.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Assuming you qualify for visa on arrival** with a UK visa or an expired US or EU visa.
- **Booking a desert safari for arrival day** after a 4 am flight.
- **Skipping Burj Khalifa pre-booking** in peak season.
- **Missing the mandir's Monday closure** on an Abu Dhabi day.
- **Planning Global Village or Miracle Garden in summer**, when they're closed.

## Who Dubai suits

**Families with kids** get theme parks, the aquarium and beaches. **Seniors** get short flights, smooth roads and wheelchair-friendly malls. **Couples** get dhow cruises, desert dinners and beach clubs. **First-time international travellers** get easy visas, Indian food and Hindi or Gujarati spoken widely.
