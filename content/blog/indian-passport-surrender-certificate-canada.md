Taking Canadian citizenship is a proud moment — but it also starts a small piece of Indian paperwork that many new citizens put off until it suddenly blocks a trip home. If you want an **OCI card** or an **Indian visa**, you will almost always need a **surrender certificate** for your old Indian passport first.

> **Why it matters:** India does not allow dual citizenship. When you voluntarily become a Canadian citizen, your Indian citizenship ends, and you must surrender your Indian passport. The surrender certificate is your proof — and the OCI application asks for it.

## Who needs a surrender certificate?

You need one if you:

- were an Indian citizen holding an Indian passport, **and**
- have since **acquired Canadian citizenship**, **and**
- now want an **OCI card**, an **Indian visa**, or simply to close the loop correctly.

Children who held Indian passports and became Canadian citizens with their parents usually need one too.

## Documents you'll typically need

- Your **last Indian passport** (original) — the certificate cannot be issued without it
- Your **Canadian passport** and **Canadian citizenship certificate** (or proof of the date you became a citizen)
- The **online application form** and photographs as specified
- Proof of **address** in your consular jurisdiction
- Any additional documents listed on the BLS checklist (for example, if your name has changed)

Always print the checklist for **your** jurisdiction — Ottawa, Toronto and Vancouver each publish their own. Nova Scotia and the rest of Atlantic Canada fall under **Toronto**, with a BLS centre in Halifax.

## Step-by-step

1. **Check your jurisdiction** on the BLS website using your postal code.
2. **Fill the online surrender application** and print it.
3. **Prepare the document bundle** — originals plus photocopies.
4. **Submit through BLS** (in person, or by post where your jurisdiction still allows it for this service) with the fee.
5. **Receive the surrender certificate** — keep the original safe; you'll upload/produce it for OCI.

## Fees and penalties

BLS's Ottawa page lists the surrender fee at **CAD 59** plus a small **BLS service fee** for the standard case. The Toronto Consulate publishes a separate table that shows a **higher fee for some people who acquired foreign citizenship on or after 1 June 2010**. Penalties can also apply if the passport wasn't surrendered in time or was used after taking Canadian citizenship, and BLS notes that any penalty **cannot be waived or reduced**.

Because these figures are revised, **confirm the current amount on the official pages** (linked below) — or ask us to check for your exact dates.

## Surrender certificate, then OCI

Once you have the surrender certificate, you can apply for your OCI card. Remember that since April 2026, **OCI applications in Canada must be submitted in person** for biometrics. Our step-by-step [OCI card guide for Canada](/blog/oci-card-application-canada) walks through it.

## Avoid these delays

- **Lost Indian passport** — sort out the lost-passport procedure first.
- **Different name spellings** across documents with no supporting proof.
- **Wrong jurisdiction** or an outdated fee.
- **Waiting until a family emergency** — start as soon as you take your citizenship oath.
