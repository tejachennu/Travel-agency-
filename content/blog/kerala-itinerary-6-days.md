A Kerala itinerary of 6 days is just enough for the state's four big experiences: tea hills, spice country and wildlife, a night on the backwaters, and old Fort Kochi. You won't feel rushed. It's the route we recommend most to families and couples from Gujarat, and the one our travellers describe as *"very smooth from start to end"*.

> **The short version:** Fly into Kochi and drive a loop: Kochi → **Munnar** (2 nights) → **Thekkady** (1 night) → **Alleppey houseboat** (1 night) → **Kochi** (1 night), using a private cab. Go between **September and March**, book the houseboat early for December–January, and expect 3–5 hours on the road on travel days.

## Kerala itinerary 6 days at a glance

| Day | Route | Approx. distance and drive | Overnight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Kochi airport → Munnar | 110 km, 4–5 hrs | Munnar |
| 2 | Munnar sightseeing | Local drives | Munnar |
| 3 | Munnar → Thekkady | 90–100 km, 3–3.5 hrs | Thekkady |
| 4 | Thekkady → Alleppey | 140 km, about 4 hrs | Houseboat |
| 5 | Alleppey → Kochi | 55 km, about 1.5 hrs | Kochi |
| 6 | Fort Kochi → airport | 40 km, 1–1.5 hrs | — |

Hill roads are narrow and winding, so drive times matter more than kilometres. In 2026 there's also road-widening work on parts of the Kochi–Munnar highway (NH 85), and traffic is sometimes squeezed into a single lane near Munnar. That's one more reason to land in Kochi by midday on Day 1.

## Getting to Kerala from Ahmedabad

**Fly to Kochi (COK).** In 2026 IndiGo has operated a nonstop Ahmedabad–Kochi flight of about 2 hours 25 minutes. There are also many one-stop options via Mumbai, Bengaluru or Hyderabad. Schedules change by season, so check the latest timings before you fix your dates. Trains from Gujarat to Ernakulam take well over a day, which costs you a full day of a short holiday each way.

Starting and ending in Kochi lets you run the whole loop with one cab and driver, so you aren't changing vehicles in each town.

## Day-by-day plan

### Day 1: Kochi to Munnar

Your driver meets you at the airport and you head east into the Western Ghats. On the way up, stop at the **Cheeyappara** and **Valara** waterfalls next to the highway. They're at their fullest from August to November and much thinner by April. Pick up tea and spices at the roadside shops near Adimali if you like, but Munnar has more choice. Plan nothing else for the evening. A walk among the tea bushes near your hotel is enough after the drive.

### Day 2: Munnar

- **Eravikulam National Park (Rajamala):** the home of the endangered Nilgiri tahr. Forest Department buses take you from the entry point up to the viewing area. The park **closes every year for the calving season**, generally from 1 February to around the end of March or early April. Book tickets on the official site in advance, because daily numbers are limited. Entry for Indian adults is about ₹200 according to the official booking site.
- **Mattupetty Dam, Echo Point and Kundala Lake:** these sit along the same road. Combine them with the drive to **Top Station** on the Tamil Nadu border if the sky is clear.
- **Tea Museum and estate walks:** a good hour to learn how tea is plucked and processed.

Munnar is famous for **Neelakurinji**, a flower that carpets the hills roughly once every 12 years. The last mass bloom was in 2018, so don't expect it on this trip.

### Day 3: Munnar to Thekkady

Leave by 8 am and you can reach Thekkady (Kumily) before lunch. Spend the afternoon on a **spice plantation walk** to see cardamom, pepper, cinnamon and vanilla growing. If you're arriving in time, take the afternoon **boat ride on Periyar Lake** in the Periyar Tiger Reserve. Two operators run boats on the lake, the Forest Department and KTDC. Each ride lasts about 1.5 hours, with departures from early morning to mid-afternoon. Fares in recent seasons have been roughly ₹250–₹450 per adult depending on the operator. Book on the official sites and report about an hour early at the Anavachal parking area, where buses take you to the boat landing. Sightings of elephants and gaur are common, but tigers are rare.

In the evening, watch a **Kathakali** or **Kalaripayattu** show in Kumily. The make-up session before Kathakali is half the fun for children.

### Day 4: Alleppey houseboat

Drive down to Alleppey (Alappuzha) to board your **houseboat** around noon. You cruise through narrow canals, paddy fields and lakeside villages. Houseboats **anchor for the night by about 5:30 pm**, because night cruising isn't allowed so that fishermen can lay their nets. You then resume for a short cruise after breakfast and check out around 9 am. Lunch, evening tea and dinner are cooked on board.

What to ask before booking:

- **Bedrooms and air-conditioning:** on many boats the AC runs only at night. Ask for the exact hours.
- **Category:** deluxe, premium and luxury boats differ a lot in room size, bathrooms and the upper deck.
- **Private or sharing:** a family or group should always book a private boat.

### Day 5: Kochi

After a short morning cruise and breakfast, drive to **Fort Kochi**. Walk past the **Chinese fishing nets**, **St Francis Church** and **Santa Cruz Basilica**, then go on to **Mattancherry** for the Dutch Palace, the antique and spice shops of **Jew Town**, and the **Paradesi Synagogue**. Note that the synagogue is usually closed on Fridays, Saturdays and Jewish holidays, and the palace is reported to close on Fridays. Gujarati travellers often like to visit Mattancherry's historic **Jain temple**, built by the Gujarati trading community that has lived here for generations. End with a sunset walk on **Marine Drive** or a ride on the **Kochi Water Metro**.

### Day 6: Fly home

Buy banana chips, tea and spices, then transfer to the airport. From Fort Kochi allow at least 1.5 hours in traffic.

## Best time to visit Kerala

| Months | Weather | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| September – November | Green after the monsoon, some showers | Waterfalls, good value, fewer crowds |
| December – February | Dry, pleasant days; chilly nights in Munnar | Peak season for everything; book early |
| March – May | Hot and humid on the coast | Hill stations still comfortable; lower prices |
| June – August | Southwest monsoon | Lush scenery and Ayurveda; expect rain delays |

If Eravikulam matters to you, avoid February and March. For the houseboat, avoid the very heaviest monsoon weeks if anyone in your group gets seasick or anxious in rain.

## Where to stay

- **Munnar:** staying near town keeps drives short. Areas such as **Chithirapuram, Pallivasal and Anachal** offer valley and tea-estate views but add 20–40 minutes of driving each way.
- **Thekkady:** stay in **Kumily**, close to the park gate, the spice shops and the evening shows.
- **Alleppey:** the houseboat is your hotel. If you'd rather sleep on land, a lakeside resort in **Kumarakom** is a quieter alternative.
- **Kochi:** **Fort Kochi** has heritage homestays and character, while **Ernakulam / Marine Drive** is better for shopping and is closer to the airport.

## What drives the cost

We don't quote fixed prices here because Kerala costs swing with dates and choices. These are the main factors:

- **Flights:** fares from Ahmedabad climb sharply around Diwali, Christmas and New Year. Booking 6–8 weeks ahead usually helps.
- **Hotel category:** a comfortable 3-star versus a resort with a view can easily double the land cost.
- **Houseboat:** price depends on the number of bedrooms, the category, the AC hours and the date. The Christmas–New Year week is the most expensive.
- **Vehicle:** a sedan suits 2–3 people, an SUV suits 4–5, and a Tempo Traveller works for larger family groups.
- **Entry fees** (Eravikulam, the Periyar boat, shows) are a small share of the total.

## Vegetarian and Jain food in Kerala

Kerala is easier for vegetarians than many people expect. Try a **sadya** on a banana leaf, **appam with vegetable stew**, **avial**, **thoran**, **sambar** and **puttu with kadala curry**. On the houseboat, tell the crew about vegetarian or Jain requirements **when you book, not when you board**, so they can buy the right groceries. Jain meals without onion, garlic or root vegetables are very doable with notice. Munnar, Kumily and Kochi all have North Indian and Gujarati-style vegetarian restaurants as a fallback.

## Packing list

- A **light jacket or sweater** for Munnar evenings, especially from December to February
- **Umbrella or rain jacket** from June to November
- **Comfortable walking shoes** with grip for wet estate paths
- **Motion-sickness tablets** for the hill roads
- **Mosquito repellent** for the houseboat evening
- Modest clothing for churches, the synagogue and temples

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Trying to squeeze in too much.** Adding Kanyakumari or Varkala to six days turns the trip into a drive-a-thon.
- **Not booking Eravikulam tickets in advance** in peak season, or planning it during the calving closure.
- **Booking the cheapest houseboat** without checking the AC hours and bathrooms.
- **Arriving in Kochi late on Day 1.** The Munnar road in the dark isn't fun.
- **Taking elephant rides.** We suggest skipping them. The spice walks, boat rides and nature trails are better experiences anyway.
