The Statue of Unity at Ekta Nagar (formerly Kevadia) is Gujarat's favourite weekend trip, and a Statue of Unity trip from Ahmedabad or Gandhinagar is one of the easiest you can plan: one good road, one train, one night. Our travellers summed it up nicely: *"proper planning, nice hotels and yummy food."* Here is how to get the planning part right.

> **The short version:** Ekta Nagar is about **200 km (3.5–4 hours)** from Ahmedabad. The statue is **closed on Mondays** and open **8 am–6 pm, Tuesday to Sunday**. Book the **viewing gallery ticket** online on the official site (around ₹380 per adult as of late 2026), stay **one night** to catch the evening show, and use day two for the **Jungle Safari** or **Ekta Cruise**.

## Statue of Unity trip from Ahmedabad: the basics

The 182-metre statue of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel stands on an island in the Narmada, about 3 km downstream of the Sardar Sarovar Dam. Around it, Ekta Nagar has grown into a full tourism township with gardens, a zoological park, a river cruise, tented stays and evening shows. You can see the statue in half a day, but the whole area deserves more.

## How to get there

### By car or cab

The most popular option for families. Take the Ahmedabad–Vadodara Expressway (about 1.5–2 hours), then head south-east past Dabhoi towards Ekta Nagar (another 2 hours or so). From Gandhinagar, add about 30 minutes to reach the expressway. Leave by 6:30–7 am and you will be at the statue by late morning, before the queues build.

A sedan works for 3–4 people; take an SUV or Tempo Traveller for larger family groups with luggage. Inside the campus, private vehicles park at designated lots and you move between attractions by the official buses.

### By train

Ekta Nagar has its own railway station. As of 2026 the **Jan Shatabdi Express** runs from Ahmedabad Junction via Nadiad, Anand and Vadodara and takes about 3 hours, with a morning departure that reaches Ekta Nagar before 11 am. Return timings are early in the day, so the train suits a two-night stay best. Check the latest schedule on the IRCTC app before booking.

### By air

If you are coming from outside Gujarat, fly to **Vadodara** and drive about 1.5 hours. Ahmedabad airport is about 200 km away, so it only makes sense if the flights are much better.

## Statue of Unity tickets and prices

Book only on the **official portal, soutickets.in** (linked in the sources below). Look-alike websites and "concierge" sellers add their own fees. As of late 2026 the official listing shows:

| Ticket | Adult | Child | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry ticket | ~₹150 | ~₹90 | Statue campus, museum, audio-visual gallery, Valley of Flowers; **not** the viewing gallery |
| Viewing gallery | ~₹380 | ~₹230 | Everything above plus the lift up to the gallery inside the statue |
| Express entry | ~₹1,030 | ~₹1,030 | Gallery access with priority through the queues at each checkpoint |

Children under 3 generally enter free; the child rate applies to a defined age band (usually up to 15), so check the age rule at checkout. Prices are revised from time to time, so treat these as approximate.

### Which ticket should you buy?

- **Viewing gallery** is the one most visitors want. The gallery sits at about 153 metres, roughly at chest level of the statue, and looks out over the dam, the Narmada and the Satpura and Vindhya hills.
- **Entry only** suits anyone who can't manage the lift and queues, or repeat visitors.
- **Express entry** is worth it on long weekends, Diwali and New Year holidays when general queues can run long, or if you are doing a day trip and every hour counts.

### Slots and timing tips

Gallery tickets are sold in **two-hour time slots** between 8 am and 6 pm, and weekend and holiday slots sell out. Book a **morning slot (8–10 am or 10–12)**: the light is better for photos, it is cooler, and visibility is usually clearer. The gallery can be closed temporarily in bad weather or high wind.

## Monday closure and special days

The statue and most Ekta Nagar attractions (Jungle Safari, Arogya Van, Children Nutrition Park, Cactus and Butterfly Garden) are **closed on Mondays** and open Tuesday to Sunday. If you are planning a long weekend, make sure your sightseeing day isn't Monday.

**31 October (Rashtriya Ekta Diwas, Sardar Patel's birth anniversary)** brings official programmes and heavy security, and normal timings, internal buses and evening shows may be changed for the day. Diwali week and the Christmas–New Year holidays are the busiest stretches of the year, so book tickets and rooms early.

## What to see in Ekta Nagar

### At the statue

- **Viewing gallery** (with the right ticket).
- **Museum and audio-visual gallery** at the base, covering Sardar Patel's life and the integration of the princely states. Allow at least 45 minutes.
- **Valley of Flowers (Bharat Vann)**, the landscaped gardens along the approach; best in the cooler months.
- **Evening light and projection show** on the statue after sunset, every day except Monday. Show timings vary by season (usually somewhere between 7 and 8 pm), so check on the day.

### Around Ekta Nagar

- **Jungle Safari (Sardar Patel Zoological Park)** — a large, well-kept zoological park with aviaries and safari enclosures; open Tuesday to Sunday, roughly 8 am to 7 pm. Around ₹200 per adult and ₹125 per child as of 2026. Give it 2–3 hours; ideal with children.
- **Arogya Van** — about 17 acres of medicinal plants and themed gardens, open 8 am to 6 pm; around ₹50 per adult. Good for seniors and an easy 45 minutes.
- **Children Nutrition Park** — a themed park with a small toy train ride, popular with families with younger kids.
- **Cactus and Butterfly Garden** — a large glasshouse of cacti and succulents plus a butterfly garden; a quick, photogenic stop.
- **Ekta Cruise** — a boat ride on the Narmada with views of the statue; the evening dinner cruise version adds the projection show. Around ₹400 per person for the standard cruise as of late 2026.
- **Sardar Sarovar Dam** — one of India's largest dams; you see it best from the viewing gallery and from the viewpoints on the internal bus route.

All of these are bookable on the official portal, where you can also book the internal bus tickets that link them.

### On the way or nearby

Many Gujarati families add a temple stop on the drive: **Garudeshwar's Dattatreya temple** is a short drive downstream of the statue, and the **Nilkanth Dham Swaminarayan temple at Poicha** works on the Rajpipla side if you have time on the way home.

### Is a day trip worth it?

It's doable: leave by 6 am, do the gallery slot at 10–12, the museum and one more attraction, and be back by 10 pm. But you'll miss the evening show and spend 7–8 hours in the car. For seniors and young kids, the overnight plan is far more comfortable.

## Where to stay

- **Tent City Narmada and other tented camps** near the statue and dam: air-conditioned "tents" that feel like cottages, with meals and cultural evenings. This is the experience most families want; it costs more on weekends and holidays.
- **Hotels and resorts in Ekta Nagar** along the approach road: convenient for the morning slot and evening show.
- **Rajpipla and the Vadodara road**: cheaper hotels, but more driving each day.

What drives the cost: weekends and festival weeks, tent category (garden versus river view), and whether meals are included. Book 2–3 weeks ahead for Diwali, Christmas or long weekends.

## Food: vegetarian and Jain options

Gujarat is a dry state, and food at Ekta Nagar is overwhelmingly vegetarian. Expect Gujarati thalis, Punjabi and South Indian dishes at the food courts near the statue and at the resorts. Jain food (no onion, garlic or root vegetables) is commonly available at tent camps and resorts, but **tell your hotel in advance** so the kitchen plans for it. Carry water and a few snacks; the campus is large and you won't always be near a food court.

## Best time to visit

| Season | Months | What it's like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winter | October–February | Most comfortable; ideal for walking and the gallery |
| Spring/summer | March–June | Hot afternoons; do sightseeing early and late |
| Monsoon | July–September | Green valley, dam spectacular when water is released; gallery may close in heavy rain or wind |

## Tips for families, seniors and kids

- **Seniors:** lifts take you to the gallery; use the internal buses and travelators, and choose the earliest gallery slot to skip the heat.
- **Kids:** the Jungle Safari and Children Nutrition Park are the highlights. Carry a cap and a refillable bottle.
- **Couples:** the evening show and dinner cruise make a lovely second evening.
- **Security:** bags are screened at the entrance, so travel light and leave anything sharp in the car.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Planning your statue visit for a Monday.** It's closed.
- **Buying from unofficial websites.** Use soutickets.in only.
- **Booking only an entry ticket by accident.** If you want the gallery, choose the viewing gallery ticket.
- **Choosing an afternoon slot in April–June.** The heat makes queues hard work.
- **Squeezing it into a day with small kids.** The overnight plan is much calmer.
