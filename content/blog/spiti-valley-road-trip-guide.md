Spiti is the road trip people talk about for years: thousand-year-old monasteries, one of the world's highest post offices, villages perched above 4,000 metres and the moon-shaped lake of Chandratal. A Spiti Valley road trip is also one of the most demanding drives in India, so the route, the month and the pace matter more than anywhere else. Our Spiti travellers came back with the photos (and the Langza Buddha selfie!) to prove it's worth it.

> **The short version:** Do the circuit **clockwise**: Chandigarh → Shimla → Kinnaur (Kalpa) → Tabo → Kaza → Chandratal → Kunzum Pass → Atal Tunnel → Manali, in **8 days or more**. The full loop is usually open from **mid-June to late September**; Kunzum Pass typically closes between **mid-October and November**. **Indians need no permit**, only photo ID at checkposts. Hire an SUV with an experienced mountain driver.

In 2026, Kunzum was cleared by the Border Roads Organisation in the second half of May. Closing dates vary every year with the first heavy snow, so check the **Lahaul and Spiti district road status page** (linked in the sources) and ask your driver a few days before the Manali leg.

## Why go clockwise (Shimla first)?

Entering through **Kinnaur** lets the altitude rise gradually: Shimla (about 2,200 m), Kalpa (about 2,900 m), Tabo (about 3,300 m), then Kaza (about 3,800 m). Your body gets three or four days to adjust before you sleep high. Going anticlockwise from Manali takes you over Kunzum at about 4,550 m on the first big day, which is the most common cause of altitude sickness on this trip. Leaving via **Kunzum, the Atal Tunnel and Manali** gives you the dramatic finale once you're acclimatised.

## How to get there from Ahmedabad

- **To the start:** fly Ahmedabad–Chandigarh (IndiGo runs nonstop flights of under two hours), then drive about 3.5–4 hours to Shimla. Alternatively, fly to Delhi and take a cab or overnight Volvo to Shimla.
- **From the finish:** drive Manali–Chandigarh (8–10 hours) for your flight home, or fly from **Bhuntar (Kullu)**, about 50 km from Manali, which has limited and weather-sensitive flights.
- **Don't self-drive** unless you have real high-altitude experience. Hire an SUV (Innova, Scorpio or similar) with a driver who has done the Spiti circuit before.

### Places not to miss

- **Kalpa:** apple orchards and the best view of the Kinner Kailash range; stay overnight.
- **Nako:** a stone-and-mud village around a small lake, with an old monastery.
- **Tabo Monastery:** founded over a thousand years ago, with remarkable murals in dim mud-walled halls. Carry a torch and don't use flash.
- **Dhankar:** the old monastery clings to a ridge above the Spiti–Pin confluence.
- **Key Monastery:** the postcard of Spiti, a white fortress-like gompa above the river.
- **Langza:** the large Buddha statue and fossil-rich slopes.
- **Hikkim:** one of the world's highest post offices; send a postcard home.
- **Chandratal:** a crescent lake at about 4,300 m. Camps are set back from the lake; you walk or drive the last stretch. The lake road usually opens a week or two after Kunzum and camps close around late September or early October.

## Atal Tunnel and permits

### Atal Tunnel

The 9.02 km **Atal Tunnel** under the Pir Panjal range connects Manali to Lahaul year-round and bypasses Rohtang Pass. On the Spiti circuit you use it on the last day, coming from Batal and Gramphu down to Manali. **No Rohtang permit is needed** to use the tunnel or travel to Lahaul and Spiti. The Rohtang permit (booked online on the Himachal government portal) is only for vehicles going up to Rohtang Pass as a sightseeing trip.

### Permits for Indians and foreigners

- **Indian citizens:** no Inner Line Permit is needed. You'll stop at police checkposts (such as those at the Kinnaur–Spiti border) to register, so carry **original government photo ID** for everyone, including children's Aadhaar.
- **Foreign nationals** (including OCI holders travelling on a foreign passport): a protected area permit is needed for the stretch close to the Tibet border between Kinnaur and Spiti. It's issued by district authorities in places such as Reckong Peo, Kaza and Shimla. Check the current process with the district before you go.

## Where to stay

- **Kalpa and Reckong Peo:** mid-range hotels with valley views.
- **Nako and Tabo:** small guesthouses and homestays; simple but warm.
- **Kaza:** the widest choice, from hotels to cosy homestays. **Homestays in Kibber or Langza** are memorable, but sleep at Kaza first to acclimatise.
- **Chandratal:** designated tented camps with shared or attached washrooms; nights are close to freezing even in summer.

What drives the cost: the SUV and driver for 8–10 days (the biggest single cost), the month (September and long weekends cost more), homestay versus hotel, and flights to Chandigarh. Ask us for a quote based on your group size.

## Food: vegetarian and Jain options

Spiti is easy for vegetarians. Homestays and dhabas serve **dal, rice, roti, sabzi, rajma**, plus **momos, thukpa** and the trip's unofficial fuel, **Maggi**. Try **seabuckthorn juice** and butter tea once. Jain food needs planning: no-onion, no-garlic cooking is possible in homestays if you ask the night before, but carry **thepla, khakhra, dry fruit and ready-to-eat meals**, especially for Chandratal and long drive days.

## Altitude and health tips

- **Hydrate:** 3–4 litres of water a day.
- **No alcohol or smoking** for the first days at altitude.
- **Sleep low, climb high:** don't stay overnight in Kibber, Langza or Chandratal before a night in Kaza.
- **Know the symptoms:** headache, nausea, breathlessness at rest or confusion mean stop climbing; descend if symptoms worsen.
- Ask your doctor about **altitude medication** before you go, and carry personal medicines in your day bag.
- **Seniors and anyone with heart, lung or BP conditions** should get medical advice first. Very young children may find Kaza's altitude hard; we usually suggest waiting until kids are older.

## Packing list

- **Warm layers** even in July: thermals, fleece, a down jacket for Chandratal
- **Sunglasses, sunscreen and lip balm**, as the UV is intense
- **Sturdy shoes**, a cap and woollen hat, gloves
- **Cash**: ATMs are few beyond Reckong Peo and the ones in Kaza can run dry
- **Power bank** and offline maps
- A basic **medical kit**, plus motion-sickness tablets for the winding Kinnaur roads

## Practical tips

- **Mobile network** is patchy: BSNL and Jio work in Kaza and some villages; expect no signal at Chandratal and Batal.
- **Fuel up** at Reckong Peo and Kaza; don't count on fuel between Kaza and Manali.
- **Start early**: streams on the Batal–Kunzum road swell in the afternoon as snow melts.
- **Respect monasteries**: ask before photographing monks, and walk clockwise around stupas.
