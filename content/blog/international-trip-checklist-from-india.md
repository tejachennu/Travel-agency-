Your first international trip is exciting and a little nerve-racking. After planning holidays for hundreds of first-time travellers from Gandhinagar and Ahmedabad, this is the first international trip checklist we share with every guest, arranged in the order you'll actually need it: documents first, then money, packing, the airport and coming home.

> **The short version:** Check your **passport has 6+ months' validity**, sort the **visa or e-visa** 4 to 6 weeks out, fill any **free digital arrival card** in the last few days, buy **travel insurance**, load a **forex card** (and know the **TCS rules**), carry **no more than USD 3,000 in foreign cash**, reach the airport **3 hours early**, and remember the **₹75,000 duty-free limit** when you come home.

## Your first international trip timeline

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 2 to 3 months before | Check passport validity; renew or apply if needed |
| 6 to 8 weeks before | Book flights and hotels; check visa rules |
| 4 to 6 weeks before | Apply for the visa or e-visa; buy travel insurance |
| 2 weeks before | Buy forex, enable international usage on one card, plan roaming or eSIM |
| 3 days before | File the arrival card (TDAC, MDAC, SG Arrival Card, IMUGA) |
| 1 day before | Web check-in, pack, print one set of documents |
| Travel day | Reach the airport 3 hours early |

## 1. Passport

- **Six months' validity** from your arrival date and at least **two blank pages**. Some countries accept less, but airlines often refuse boarding below six months.
- Your name must match **exactly** on your passport, visa and tickets. Watch out for initials, surnames and spelling changes after marriage.
- New or renewed passports are booked through **Passport Seva**. In Gujarat, appointments are handled through the Ahmedabad Regional Passport Office and the Passport Seva Kendras and Post Office PSKs across the state. Police verification can take a couple of weeks, so start early. **Tatkaal** is available for urgent cases at a higher fee.
- Newer Indian passports are **e-passports** with a chip. Older passports remain valid until they expire.
- Take a **photo of your passport's photo page** and save it offline and in email.

## 2. Visa, eTA and entry rules

Rules vary widely and change often. Here's where things stand for Indian passport holders as of October 2026:

| Country | What you need |
| --- | --- |
| Thailand | Visa-exempt up to 30 days + free TDAC ([guide](/blog/thailand-visa-for-indians)) |
| Malaysia | Visa-free up to 30 days until 31 Dec 2026 + free MDAC |
| Singapore | Visa through an authorised agent + free SG Arrival Card ([guide](/blog/singapore-malaysia-family-itinerary)) |
| Dubai (UAE) | Pre-approved e-visa for most travellers ([guide](/blog/dubai-trip-from-ahmedabad)) |
| Vietnam | E-visa ([guide](/blog/vietnam-e-visa-for-indians)) |
| Bali (Indonesia) | Visa on arrival or e-VoA + Bali tourist levy |
| Maldives | Free visa on arrival + IMUGA declaration ([guide](/blog/maldives-honeymoon-guide)) |
| Sri Lanka | Online ETA before travel (fee waived for Indians since May 2026) |
| Nepal and Bhutan | No visa; Bhutan needs an entry permit and SDF ([guide](/blog/bhutan-trip-from-india-guide)) |

Confirm the rules on the official government website before you book non-refundable flights, and again a week before travel.

### What is an eTA?

An **electronic travel authorisation** (eTA or ETA) is a quick online approval linked to your passport. It is meant for travellers who **don't need a full visa**. Sri Lanka uses one for Indians. Most Indian passport holders **aren't eligible** for Canada's eTA, the UK's ETA or the US ESTA, and need a proper visa for those countries instead. If a website offers an "eTA" for a country where Indians need a visa, treat it as a red flag.

### Digital arrival cards

Many countries now ask you to fill a **free online arrival form** in the days before you land: **TDAC** (Thailand), **MDAC** (Malaysia), **SG Arrival Card** (Singapore) and **IMUGA** (Maldives). Each traveller, including children, needs their own. Use only the official government portal. Copycat sites charge for forms that are free.

## 3. Bookings to keep offline

Save PDFs of your **e-tickets, hotel vouchers, visa, insurance policy and arrival-card confirmations** on your phone, email them to yourself and **print one set**. Immigration officers often ask for your return ticket and first hotel address, and a dead phone battery is no excuse.

## 4. Money, forex and TCS

### How to carry money

- A **multi-currency forex card** for most spending. Rates are locked when you load it, and it's safer than cash.
- **Some local cash** for the first day: taxis, tips, a SIM card.
- One **debit or credit card enabled for international use** as a backup. Check its foreign-currency markup fee, which is often a few percent plus GST.
- Avoid exchanging money at the destination airport, where rates are usually poor.

### Cash limits (RBI)

- **Foreign currency notes:** up to **USD 3,000 or equivalent per trip** for most countries. Anything more should be on a forex card.
- **Indian rupees:** up to **₹25,000** in cash.
- **Coming back:** declare foreign currency on arrival if your **cash exceeds USD 5,000**, or **cash plus forex cards and cheques exceeds USD 10,000**.

### LRS and TCS in 2026

Under the RBI's **Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)**, each resident can send or spend up to **USD 250,000 a financial year** abroad. Tax collected at source (TCS) applies on top, and the rules changed on **1 April 2026**:

| What you're paying for | TCS from 1 April 2026 |
| --- | --- |
| Overseas tour package from an Indian operator | 2%, no threshold |
| Forex or remittances for other purposes (including forex for your own trip) | Nil up to ₹10 lakh a financial year, 20% above |
| Education or medical treatment abroad | Nil up to ₹10 lakh, 2% above |

**TCS is not an extra cost.** It's tax paid in advance against your PAN. It shows in your Form 26AS and AIS, and you can adjust it against your tax or claim it back as a refund when you file your income tax return. Keep the invoices. Your tour operator or forex provider will need your PAN.

## 5. Travel insurance

Buy a policy covering **medical emergencies (including hospitalisation), trip delays and cancellations, lost baggage and passport loss**. It's **mandatory for Schengen visas** and some others, and essential everywhere. Seniors should declare existing conditions and check the age-based limits. Save the insurer's **24/7 emergency number** in your phone.

## 6. Health and medicines

- Carry prescription medicines in **original packaging**, with a copy of the prescription.
- Some medicines that are common in India are restricted elsewhere (certain painkillers, sleeping pills and cough syrups). Check before you fly to the UAE, Singapore or Japan.
- A **yellow fever certificate** is required if you're travelling from or through parts of Africa or South America.
- Pack a small kit: paracetamol, ORS, antacids, motion-sickness tablets, band-aids.

## 7. Phone and apps

- An **international roaming pack** or a **local eSIM**. Buy eSIMs before you leave, and check your phone supports them.
- **Offline maps**, a **translator** app, your airline's app and a ride-hailing app for the destination (Grab in Southeast Asia, Careem or Uber in Dubai).
- WhatsApp works almost everywhere. Keep your travel agent's number handy; ours is available around the clock while you travel.

## 8. Packing smart

- **Check your baggage allowance.** Budget airlines are strict on weight and number of bags.
- **Liquids in cabin bags:** containers of 100 ml or less, in one clear resealable bag.
- **Power banks** go in **cabin baggage only**, never in checked bags. Since January 2026, India's DGCA has banned using them on board, and they shouldn't go in overhead bins.
- **Universal adapter**. Plugs differ (UK-style three-pin in Singapore, Malaysia and Dubai).
- **Food:** dry snacks such as thepla and khakhra are usually fine, but declare food where asked. Australia and New Zealand, for example, are strict.
- **Don't pack** vapes or e-cigarettes for Singapore or Thailand, or alcohol, pork or idols of worship for the Maldives.

## 9. At the airport

- Reach **3 hours before** an international flight. Counters typically close about an hour before departure.
- Keep your **passport, boarding pass and visa** in one pouch.
- **FTI-TTP (fast-track immigration):** Indian citizens and OCI cardholders can register free on the Ministry of Home Affairs portal and use automated e-gates at immigration. **Ahmedabad** is one of the airports covered. Registration lasts five years or until your passport expires, whichever comes first.
- **Digi Yatra:** India's face-recognition entry system is widely used for **domestic** flights, including at Ahmedabad. For international departures, it's only being trialled at a few major airports, so plan to use your passport. If your trip starts with a domestic leg (for example, Ahmedabad to Mumbai), you can use Digi Yatra for that leg.
- Fill any immigration forms honestly, and keep your first hotel address ready.

## 10. Coming home: customs and duty-free

Under India's **Baggage Rules 2026** (in force from 2 February 2026), Indian residents arriving by air or sea get a **general duty-free allowance of ₹75,000** for new goods and gifts, in addition to used personal effects. One laptop is allowed separately for passengers aged 18 and above. Alcohol, tobacco and gold jewellery have their own limits, so check the CBIC rules before buying in bulk. If you're over the limit, use the **red channel** and pay the duty. Getting caught in the green channel is far more expensive.

## Mistakes first-time travellers make

- **Booking flights before checking the visa** and losing money on non-refundable fares.
- **Name mismatches** between passport and ticket.
- **Paying a fee on a fake arrival-card website.**
- **Carrying too much cash** and not declaring it.
- **Skipping insurance** to save a little money.
- **Packing the power bank in checked luggage.**
