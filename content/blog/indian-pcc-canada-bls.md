Sooner or later, most Indians building a life in Canada are asked for a police clearance certificate from India. It might be for permanent residence, a job offer or a visa for a third country. You don't need to fly home or ask relatives to queue at a passport office. An **Indian PCC in Canada** is issued by the High Commission or the Consulates and collected through BLS International.

> **The short version:** Fill the PCC form online on the Global Passport Seva portal, then submit the signed printout with your **valid passport**, proof of status and address in Canada, photos and the fee at a **BLS centre in your jurisdiction**. As of October 2026, the fee is about **CAD 60.70** (CAD 59 consular + CAD 1.70 BLS) in the Toronto and Vancouver jurisdictions. Allow **around 8 weeks**, because the certificate is only issued after police in India send a clear report. **Nova Scotia is under the Toronto Consulate**, and BLS has a centre in Halifax.

## What is an Indian PCC?

A Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) is an official statement from the Government of India that there is nothing adverse on record against you. When you're abroad, the Indian mission issues it after checking with the police for your address in India. It's not the same as a "character certificate" from a local police station or a gazetted officer. IRCC specifically says character certificates are **not accepted**.

From Canada, applications go through **BLS International**, the outsourced service provider for the High Commission of India in Ottawa and the Consulates in Toronto and Vancouver. The mission makes the decision and issues the certificate. BLS accepts your file, collects the fee and returns the certificate.

## Who needs an Indian PCC in Canada?

The most common reasons we see:

- **Permanent residence.** IRCC generally wants a police certificate from every country where you lived for six months or more in a row since turning 18. For most Indian applicants that includes India. For a country you no longer live in, the certificate must have been issued **after** you last lived there, so a PCC applied for from Canada fits that rule.
- **Canadian citizenship.** If you spent 183 days or more in a row in another country (since age 18) during the four years before applying, IRCC asks for a police certificate from that country. There's an exception for time in your country of origin immediately before you became a PR.
- **Jobs and licensing.** Some employers, regulated professions and volunteer roles ask for an Indian PCC alongside a Canadian background check.
- **Other immigration.** Visas or residence in the USA, Australia, New Zealand, the UK or the Gulf often need one. So can adoption and some long-stay visas.

**Express Entry tip:** after an Invitation to Apply you have 60 days to submit your application. A PCC that takes 8 weeks leaves very little margin, so apply as soon as you enter the pool. If it still hasn't arrived, IRCC accepts a letter of explanation plus proof that you applied (your BLS receipt and tracking). That's at the officer's discretion, so don't plan around it.

## Which consulate covers you?

Your jurisdiction depends on where you live in Canada, not where you came from in India. Using the wrong one is a common cause of returned files.

| Mission | Covers (check by postal code on BLS) |
| --- | --- |
| High Commission of India, Ottawa | National Capital Region, Ontario postal codes starting with K, Quebec postal codes starting with H or J |
| Consulate General of India, Toronto | Rest of Ontario, Manitoba, **Nova Scotia**, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador |
| Consulate General of India, Vancouver | British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Yukon, Northwest Territories |

**Nova Scotia residents:** you're under the **Toronto Consulate**, not Ottawa. BLS lists a Halifax centre (998 Parkland Drive, Unit 101A, as of 2026) under the Toronto jurisdiction, alongside centres in the GTA and Winnipeg. That means families in Chester, Lunenburg, Bridgewater or the Annapolis Valley can submit in Halifax instead of travelling to Ontario. Confirm the address and hours on the BLS site before you drive in.

## How to apply for an Indian PCC through BLS

### 1. Fill the online form

The Toronto Consulate says the PCC application must be filled and submitted online on the **Global Passport Seva (GPSP) portal**, the Passport Seva system for Indians abroad. You can't make manual changes after you submit, so check every detail against your passport first: name spelling, date and place of birth, parents' names and Indian address.

Print the completed application and sign it where indicated.

### 2. Gather documents

Print the **BLS PCC checklist for your jurisdiction** and work through it. Typical documents are listed in the next section.

### 3. Submit at BLS: walk-in or post

- **Walk-in (recommended).** The Indian missions say walk-ins are processed faster. Bring originals and self-attested copies. Walk-in fees have been payable by cash or debit at the counter.
- **By post.** Postal applications have been accepted, paid by bank draft or money order to BLS International. The missions warn that they take longer. Since April 2026 OCI and visa applications must be made in person for biometrics. We haven't seen the same rule announced for PCCs, but check the BLS notices page before you post anything.

### 4. Police verification in India

The mission sends your details to the police authorities for your address in India. A PCC is issued **only after a "clear" report comes back**. This is the step that controls your timeline, and nobody in Canada can speed it up.

### 5. Collection

Track your application on the BLS site. When the certificate is ready, collect it or have it couriered, depending on what you chose and paid for.

## Documents you'll need

What you need depends on whether you still hold an Indian passport.

**Indian passport holders**

- **Original valid Indian passport** plus copies of the first and last pages, and of pages with Indian entry and exit stamps if the checklist asks for them
- **Signed GPSP application printout**
- **Proof of status in Canada**: PR card, work permit, study permit or visitor record. If your permit has expired and you're on maintained status, include proof that you applied to extend it.
- **Proof of address** in your jurisdiction, such as a driver's licence, recent bank statement or utility bill
- **Photographs** to BLS specifications, in the number the checklist asks for
- Any **declaration or reference forms** on the checklist

**Canadian (or other foreign) citizens who were Indian citizens**

- **Current Canadian passport** (original and copy)
- **Cancelled old Indian passport** and/or surrender certificate, as the checklist requires
- Proof of Canadian citizenship, proof of address, photos and the signed application
- For a PCC covering a **stay in India as a foreigner**: the passport you travelled on and copies of the Indian entry and exit stamps or visa

If you don't have a document the checklist asks for, the Toronto BLS page says to get prior approval by emailing the Consulate's PCC desk (pcc.toronto@mea.gov.in) **before** you submit.

## Indian PCC fees in Canada

Indian missions in Canada revised consular fees from **1 July 2026**. Applications made with the old fee are not accepted.

| Item | Amount (as of October 2026) |
| --- | --- |
| Consular fee: PCC | CAD 59 |
| BLS service fee | CAD 1.70 |
| **Total at the counter** | **about CAD 60.70** |
| Return courier | Extra, if you choose it |

These are the figures published for the **Toronto** and **Vancouver** jurisdictions. BLS lists the same total for Indian passport holders and Canadian or other foreign passport holders. One BLS Ottawa page we checked still showed an older, lower total, so Ottawa-jurisdiction applicants should confirm the live figure before paying.

## How long does it take?

| Stage | Typical time |
| --- | --- |
| Online form and document prep | 1–3 evenings |
| BLS appointment or walk-in | Depends on slot availability at your centre |
| Postal submission | Adds transit time each way |
| Mission and police verification in India | Around **8 weeks**, no guarantee |

The Toronto Consulate puts the process at **around 8 weeks** and says there is no guarantee. Some applicants get theirs faster, but don't count on it.

### Why police verification delays happen

- The **address in India** on your passport is old, so the family has moved or the house is locked up.
- **Nobody is home** when police call or visit, or relatives don't know the application exists.
- **Name or date mismatches** between your passport, application and Indian records.
- A **pending case** or adverse entry, even a minor or old one, which needs clarification.

Tell your family in India that police may call or visit about your PCC, and make sure someone can confirm you lived there.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Applying while your passport is being renewed.** The Toronto Consulate accepts PCC applications only with a valid passport, not with a re-issue application.
- **Details that don't match your passport.** If your name, address or marital status has changed, the Consulate says to re-issue the passport with current particulars first. Our [Indian passport renewal guide](/blog/indian-passport-renewal-canada) covers that.
- **Wrong jurisdiction.** Atlantic Canada residents often assume Ottawa. It's Toronto.
- **Paying the pre-July 2026 fee.** The application will be returned.
- **Bringing a character certificate instead.** IRCC won't accept it.
- **Leaving it too late** for an Express Entry or job deadline. Start as soon as you know you'll need one.
- **Photos not to spec.** The usual problems are a background that isn't white, shadows or the wrong size.

## PCC, passport, OCI: what comes first?

If you're still an Indian citizen on a work permit or PR, the order is usually: keep your **passport valid** (renew first if needed), then apply for the **PCC**. Once you take Canadian citizenship, you'll need an [Indian passport surrender certificate](/blog/indian-passport-surrender-certificate-canada) and then an [OCI card](/blog/oci-card-application-canada) for hassle-free trips home.
