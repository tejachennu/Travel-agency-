Singapore and Malaysia together make the perfect "first big family trip" from India: two countries, short hops between them, theme parks for the kids and skylines for the parents. This Singapore Malaysia family itinerary covers seven days at an easy pace, with practical notes on visas, transfers, vegetarian food and where to stay.

> **The short version:** Spend **4 nights in Singapore** (city, Gardens by the Bay, Sentosa and Universal Studios, Night Safari) and **2 nights in Kuala Lumpur** (Petronas Towers, Batu Caves, Genting Highlands), linked by a one-hour flight. Indians need a **Singapore visa** through an authorised agent plus the free **SG Arrival Card**; **Malaysia is visa-free for up to 30 days until 31 December 2026**, with the free **MDAC** filed within three days before arrival.

## Visas and arrival cards for Indians

Sort this first. The Singapore visa is the only step that needs real lead time.

| | Singapore | Malaysia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visa for Indians | Required, through an authorised visa agent | Not required for up to 30 days (exemption runs to 31 Dec 2026) |
| Typical processing | About 3 working days after the agent submits | Not applicable |
| Arrival card | SG Arrival Card, free, within 3 days before arrival | MDAC, free, within 3 days before arrival |
| Passport | Valid 6+ months | Valid 6+ months |
| Purpose | Tourism | Tourism, social visits; not work or study |

### Singapore visa

Indian passport holders on ordinary passports must have a visa before travelling. Applications go through an **authorised visa agent** listed by the Singapore mission (Gujarat residents usually fall under the Mumbai consulate). You pay the government fee plus the agent's service charge. Check the current total with the agent, as it varies by mission. Processing is usually a few working days, but apply **three to four weeks before travel** so a request for extra documents doesn't derail the trip. Children need their own application.

Typical documents: passport, recent photographs to the specified size, the visa application form, confirmed return tickets, hotel bookings, recent bank statements and, for children, a birth certificate.

### SG Arrival Card

Every traveller, including infants, must submit the **SG Arrival Card** on the ICA website or the MyICA app **within three days before arrival** (including the arrival day). It's free. The SG Arrival Card is not a visa; your **e-Pass**, which confirms how long you can stay, is emailed to the address you enter, so use one you can check.

### Malaysia visa exemption and MDAC

Malaysia extended its visa exemption for Indian nationals to **31 December 2026**, allowing stays of **up to 30 days** for purposes other than work or study. As of mid-October 2026, no further extension has been announced, so if you're travelling in **January 2027 or later**, check the latest position before booking. Everyone must still file the **Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC)** on the official Immigration Department portal **within three days before arrival**. Each person needs their own form; group submissions aren't accepted.

Use only official websites for both cards. Look-alike sites charge for forms that are free.

## Getting there from Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad has had **non-stop flights to both Singapore and Kuala Lumpur** (overnight departures of roughly five and a half hours), but operating days change by season. Mumbai has many more daily options to both cities. The best plan is an **open-jaw ticket**: fly into Singapore and home from Kuala Lumpur, so you never backtrack.

### Day 1: Arrive in Singapore

Most flights from India land early in the morning, so book an **early check-in** or plan a gentle first day. If the kids are awake, **Jewel Changi** and its indoor waterfall, the Rain Vortex, is right at the airport. In the evening, walk the **Marina Bay** promenade and watch **Spectra**, the free light-and-water show outside Marina Bay Sands (usually 8pm and 9pm, with an extra 10pm show on Fridays and Saturdays).

### Day 2: City and Gardens by the Bay

Start at **Merlion Park** for the classic photo, then **Chinatown** (Buddha Tooth Relic Temple) and lunch in **Little India**. In the afternoon, visit the two cooled conservatories at **Gardens by the Bay**: the **Flower Dome** and the **Cloud Forest** with its indoor waterfall. Stay for **Garden Rhapsody**, the free Supertree light show at 7:45pm and 8:45pm.

### Day 3: Sentosa and Universal Studios

Take the **Sentosa Express** from VivoCity. **Universal Studios Singapore** is a full day. Younger children love **Minion Land** and Madagascar, and older ones head for the Transformers and Battlestar Galactica rides. Arrive at opening, ride the big attractions first and use the app for wait times. End with **Wings of Time** on Siloso Beach after dark.

### Day 4: Oceanarium and Night Safari

A slower morning: the hotel pool, or the **Singapore Oceanarium** at Resorts World Sentosa (the bigger successor to the old S.E.A. Aquarium, opened in 2025). Alternatively, visit **Bird Paradise** at Mandai. After an early dinner, take the **Night Safari** tram through the zoo after dark. It's about 30 to 40 minutes from the city by cab. Kids under six may be sleepy by the end, so go for the first entry slot.

### Day 5: Singapore to Kuala Lumpur

Fly to KL (about an hour in the air; allow half a day with transfers). Spend the evening at **KLCC Park** below the **Petronas Twin Towers**, with its free children's water play area and the musical fountain at Lake Symphony. Book the **Petronas Skybridge** in advance if you want to go up.

### Day 6: Batu Caves and Genting Highlands

Leave early for **Batu Caves**, around 30 minutes north of the city: 272 rainbow-painted steps up to a limestone temple cave, with a giant golden statue of Lord Murugan. Knees and shoulders should be covered (wraps are available at the base), and watch out for the monkeys snatching snacks. Continue to **Genting Highlands**, about an hour from Batu Caves, and ride the **Awana SkyWay** cable car up the mountain, roughly ten minutes over the rainforest. At the top it's a cool 15 to 23°C, with the indoor **Skytropolis** park and the outdoor **Genting SkyWorlds** theme park. Return to KL in the evening.

### Day 7: Shopping and fly home

Shop in **Bukit Bintang** (Pavilion, Lot 10) or Suria KLCC, then head to KLIA. Allow at least an hour to reach the airport and check whether your flight leaves from KLIA Terminal 1 or 2.

## Singapore to Kuala Lumpur: flight, coach or train?

| Option | Door-to-door time | Good for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Flight | Around 4 to 5 hours with airport time | Families with small kids, tight schedules | About an hour in the air; many daily flights |
| Coach | 5 to 6 hours, longer at peak times | Budget travellers | Border checks at the Causeway can add delays |
| Train via Johor Bahru | 6+ hours with connections | Rail fans | ETS train JB Sentral to KL takes roughly 4 to 4.5 hours |

The new RTS Link shuttle between Woodlands and Johor Bahru is now expected to open for passengers in **February 2027**, which will make the train route smoother. For most families, the flight is still the simplest choice.

## Where to stay

**Singapore:**
- **Little India, Farrer Park and Bugis:** best value, plenty of vegetarian food, good MRT access.
- **Orchard Road:** central, great for shopping and malls, family room options.
- **Marina Bay and Clarke Quay:** iconic views, pricier.
- **Sentosa:** resort-style, great if Universal Studios is the main event, but further from the city.

**Kuala Lumpur:**
- **Bukit Bintang:** walkable, lively, lots of food.
- **KLCC:** next to the Petronas Towers and the park.
- **Brickfields (KL Sentral):** KL's Little India, excellent for vegetarian food and the airport train.

Singapore hotel rooms are compact and pricey, so families of four often need an interconnecting room or a family room. Malaysian hotels charge a small **tourism tax per room per night** for foreign guests, collected at check-in.

## Vegetarian and Jain food

This is one of the easiest destinations in Asia for vegetarian families. In Singapore, **Serangoon Road** and the lanes around it in Little India are packed with South Indian, North Indian and pure-veg restaurants, and food courts (hawker centres) usually have a vegetarian stall. In KL, **Brickfields** is the go-to. At Universal Studios and in Genting, choices are limited, so carry snacks.

For **Jain** meals, carry dry snacks (theplas and khakhras travel well) and tell your hotel in advance. Indian restaurants in Little India and Brickfields can often adapt dishes (no onion, garlic or root vegetables) if you explain clearly before ordering.

## Best time to visit

Both countries are hot and humid all year (roughly 25 to 33°C), with short, heavy afternoon showers. Plan around school holidays rather than the weather.

| Period | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| October to November (Diwali) | Deepavali lights in Little India; showers become more frequent |
| December to early January | Christmas lights on Orchard Road; peak prices; wetter monsoon season |
| February to April | Usually the driest stretch; good value outside holidays |
| May to June (summer holidays) | Very popular with Indian families; hot, book early |

## Family travel tips

- **Buy theme-park tickets online** in advance for better prices.
- **Use the MRT in Singapore:** it's clean, air-conditioned and buggy-friendly. Grab cabs work well in both countries.
- **Book a pool afternoon** every couple of days; the heat tires kids quickly.
- **Don't pack vapes or e-cigarettes:** they are illegal in Singapore, with heavy fines.
- **Carry a light jacket** for Genting and the cool Gardens by the Bay domes.
- **Plug type:** both countries use UK-style three-pin sockets, so pack an adapter.
