Vietnam has become one of the most loved destinations among our travellers. The Golden Bridge at Ba Na Hills, Ha Long Bay's limestone islands, lantern-lit Hoi An and great value for money all help. The only homework is the visa. The Vietnam e-visa for Indians is a single online form on one official website, and it's easy once you know the steps and the traps.

> **The short version:** Indians need a **Vietnam e-visa** for a holiday. Apply only on **evisa.gov.vn**. It costs **USD 25 (single entry)** or **USD 50 (multiple entry)**, can be valid for **up to 90 days**, and usually takes **about 3 working days**. Allow 2 to 3 weeks before you fly.

## Do Indians need a visa for Vietnam?

Yes. India isn't on Vietnam's visa-exemption list, so Indian passport holders need a visa for mainland Vietnam. Since August 2023 Vietnam has offered e-visas to all nationalities, valid for **up to 90 days**, single or multiple entry. That covers almost every holiday our travellers take.

The one exception is **Phu Quoc island**. Foreigners who fly **directly into Phu Quoc from abroad** can stay up to 30 days without a visa, but only if they stay on the island. If you then fly to Hanoi or Da Nang, you need a valid visa for the mainland. Direct India to Phu Quoc flights have mostly been seasonal charters, so for most Gujarat travellers the e-visa is still the way to go.

## Vietnam e-visa fees for Indians

| Type | Government fee (as of late 2026) | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single entry, up to 90 days | USD 25 | A normal Vietnam-only holiday |
| Multiple entry, up to 90 days | USD 50 | Side trips to Cambodia, Laos or Thailand and back |
| Agent or look-alike website | Often USD 60 to 120+ | Avoid unless you deliberately want a paid service |

These fees were reconfirmed in Vietnam's 2026 fee circular. You pay online by international debit or credit card, and the fee is **non-refundable**, even if your application is refused or you made a mistake. Check the portal at the time of applying, because government fees can change.

## Documents you need

- **Passport** valid for at least **six months** from your arrival date, with at least two blank pages.
- **Passport data-page scan**: a clear, flat colour image of the photo page with the two lines of letters and numbers at the bottom fully readable.
- **Portrait photo**: 4×6 cm style, recent, plain white or light background, looking straight at the camera, **no spectacles**, JPG/JPEG, under about 2 MB.
- **Address in India** and your occupation details.
- **Address of your first hotel** in Vietnam.
- **Planned entry and exit ports** (airport names) and dates.
- **Return or onward flight** (not uploaded, but carry it; immigration and airlines may ask).

For children, each child needs their own e-visa application with their own passport scan and photo, even if they're travelling on a parent's booking.

## How to apply for the Vietnam e-visa, step by step

### 1. Open the official portal

Type **evisa.gov.vn** directly into your browser. Don't search for it or click ads. Vietnam's Immigration Department and the Vietnamese Embassy in New Delhi both warn that look-alike sites rank highly in search results, and some use convincing domain names.

### 2. Upload your photo and passport page

The portal reads details from your passport scan. Check every auto-filled field against your passport. Surname, given names, date of birth and passport number must match exactly.

### 3. Fill in your trip details

Choose **single or multiple entry**, then your intended **entry date** and how long the visa should last (up to 90 days). Add your **entry port** (for example Noi Bai in Hanoi, Tan Son Nhat in Ho Chi Minh City or Da Nang), your **exit port** and your first hotel's address.

### 4. Pay and save your registration code

After paying you get a **registration code**. Save it with the email you used. You need both to check your status and download the visa.

### 5. Check status and download

Check the "search" section of the portal after three working days. When it shows approved, **download the e-visa PDF, check every detail and print two copies**. Keep a copy offline on your phone too.

## How long does it take?

Officially about **three working days**. In practice, plan for a week, because:

- applications aren't processed on **weekends** or **Vietnamese public holidays**,
- the **Tet (Lunar New Year)** shutdown in late January or February causes a backlog,
- a blurry scan or mismatched name can lead to a request to apply again.

We file our travellers' e-visas **two to three weeks** before departure, so there's time to fix a problem without changing flights.

## Single or multiple entry?

- **Single entry** suits a Vietnam-only trip, like Hanoi, Ha Long Bay, Da Nang and Hoi An.
- **Multiple entry** is worth the extra USD 25 if you'll leave and come back. Common examples are a few days at **Angkor Wat in Cambodia**, a hop to **Luang Prabang in Laos**, or flying out to **Bangkok** and back to Vietnam for your flight home.

A single-entry visa ends when you leave Vietnam, even if days remain.

## At the airport in Vietnam

Carry your **printed e-visa**, passport, return ticket and hotel booking. Enter through the **port named on your e-visa** to avoid questions.

Vietnam has also started a free **online pre-arrival declaration**. It began as a pilot at Ho Chi Minh City's Tan Son Nhat airport in April 2026 and is being extended to other airports. You submit it in the three days before you land and get a QR code to show at immigration. It doesn't replace the e-visa. Check the latest on the Immigration Department's site or with your airline before you fly; we confirm it for our travellers along with the final documents.

## Common mistakes Indians make

- **Paying a look-alike website.** If the fee is far above USD 25 or USD 50, you're not on the government site.
- **Swapping name fields.** Your **surname** goes in the surname field, exactly as printed. If your passport has only a given name, follow the portal's guidance rather than guessing.
- **Wrong date of birth or passport number.** One wrong digit can mean being refused boarding. There's no paid correction service, so you would have to apply and pay again.
- **Using a selfie or the passport photo itself as the portrait.** Use a fresh, front-facing photo with a white background and no glasses.
- **Entry date too late.** You can't enter before the visa's start date, so set it a day earlier if your flight lands after midnight.
- **Choosing single entry for a Cambodia side trip.** You'd need a new visa to come back.
- **Overstaying.** Vietnam fines overstays per day, and it can affect future visas.

## Best time to visit Vietnam

Vietnam is long and thin, so the weather is different in the north, centre and south.

| Region | Most comfortable months | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| North (Hanoi, Ha Long Bay, Sapa) | October to April | Cool, misty winters from December to February; carry a jacket |
| Centre (Da Nang, Hoi An, Hue) | February to August | Heavy rain and storm risk from about September to December |
| South (Ho Chi Minh City, Mekong, Phu Quoc) | December to April | Short tropical downpours from May to October |

For a north-plus-centre trip, **March to May** is a sweet spot. Our families from Gujarat also like Diwali holidays for Hanoi and Ha Long, but check the weather in central Vietnam before adding Da Nang that late in the year.

## Vegetarian and Jain food in Vietnam

Vietnam is easier for vegetarians than most people expect. Look for the word **"chay"** (vegetarian). Buddhist vegetarian restaurants serve tofu, mushroom and vegetable dishes, fresh spring rolls and vegetarian pho. Hanoi's Old Quarter, Da Nang, Hoi An and Ho Chi Minh City all have Indian restaurants with thali and Jain-friendly options on request.

Watch for **fish sauce**, which is used widely, so say "no fish sauce" clearly. Jain travellers should carry theplas, khakhra and ready-to-eat packs for cruise days, where menus are fixed. Tell us in advance and we request vegetarian or Jain meals on Ha Long cruises and at hotels.

## Money and costs

The currency is the **Vietnamese dong (VND)**. Carry some **US dollars** to exchange on arrival, use a forex card for hotels and malls, and keep small notes for markets and taxis.

What drives your trip cost:

- **flight route and season** (Diwali and December are peak),
- **Ha Long cruise category** (day boat vs overnight cruise),
- **hotel area**: Hanoi Old Quarter, Da Nang beachfront and central Hoi An cost more,
- **number of internal flights**.

Vietnam is still a good-value international trip compared with Singapore or Dubai.

- **Families with kids**: Ba Na Hills' Fantasy Park, VinWonders and beach days. Keep transfers short.
- **Seniors**: very doable with a slower pace. Choose day cruises and use cable cars rather than long walks.
