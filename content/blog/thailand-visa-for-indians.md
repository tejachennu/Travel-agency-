Thailand is still one of the easiest international holidays for Indian travellers — direct flights from Ahmedabad and Mumbai, no visa queues for a short trip, and beaches, temples and shopping within a few hours of each other. But one big rule changed in September 2026, and many older blogs haven't caught up.

> **The short version (as of October 2026):** Indian passport holders can visit Thailand visa-free for up to **30 days**. Fill in the free **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** in the three days before you land, carry a return ticket, and make sure your passport has at least six months of validity.

## What changed in 2026?

Since late 2023, Indians had enjoyed a generous visa exemption that eventually allowed stays of up to 60 days. In August 2026 the Thai government published a notification cutting the visa-exempt stay for Indian passport holders to **30 days**, effective **15 September 2026**.

For most holidaymakers this changes nothing — a typical Bangkok–Pattaya or Phuket–Krabi trip is 5 to 8 nights. It matters if you were planning a long stay, a workation or back-to-back island hopping for more than a month.

## Who can travel visa-free?

You can use the visa exemption if you:

- hold an **ordinary Indian passport** valid for at least six months from your arrival date,
- are travelling for **tourism, visiting friends or a short business meeting**,
- have a **confirmed return or onward ticket** within 30 days,
- can show **proof of accommodation** and enough funds for your stay if asked.

Immigration officers can ask for any of these at the counter, so keep hotel bookings and your return e-ticket offline on your phone.

## How to fill the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)

The TDAC replaced the old paper arrival card. It is **free** and takes about ten minutes.

1. Open the official TDAC website (linked in the sources below) — avoid look-alike sites that charge a fee.
2. Enter your passport details exactly as printed, your flight number and arrival date.
3. Add your first night's accommodation address and your travel history for the last 14 days.
4. Submit and save the confirmation (a QR code/PDF) on your phone.

You can submit it **within the three days before arrival** (including the arrival day). If you're travelling as a family, each traveller needs their own entry — we usually file these for our guests along with the itinerary.

## What to carry at the airport

| Document | Why you need it |
| --- | --- |
| Passport (6+ months validity, blank pages) | Mandatory for entry |
| TDAC confirmation | Checked at immigration |
| Return / onward e-ticket within 30 days | Proof you'll leave on time |
| Hotel bookings | Proof of accommodation |
| Some cash or a forex card | Officers may ask about funds |
| Travel insurance | Strongly recommended — medical costs in Thailand are high |

## Can you extend a Thailand stay beyond 30 days?

Usually, yes — once. You can apply at a Thai immigration office for an extension of up to 30 days, commonly for a fee of 1,900 baht. Approval is discretionary, so don't build an itinerary that depends on it. If you know you'll stay longer than a month, apply for the appropriate visa before you fly.

## Best time to visit Thailand from India

The **cool, dry season from November to April** is the most comfortable time for Bangkok, Pattaya and the Andaman coast (Phuket, Krabi, Phi Phi). May to October brings monsoon showers on the west coast, but also lower hotel prices and quieter beaches — the Gulf islands such as Koh Samui are often a better bet then.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Using a paid "TDAC agent" website.** The form is free on the official portal.
- **Booking a one-way ticket.** Without an onward ticket you may be refused boarding or entry.
- **Over-staying by "just a day".** Overstay fines are charged per day and can affect future visits.
- **Relying on old blog posts** that still mention 60 days.
