An Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) card is the closest thing to a lifelong visa for India: multiple entries, no time limit on visits, and no need to apply for a fresh visa every time you fly home to Gujarat. If you're an Indian-origin Canadian, it's usually the single most useful document you can hold.

The process has one important change in 2026, so read this before you book a slot.

> **What's new (2026):** Since **5 April 2026**, OCI applications in Canada must be **submitted in person at a BLS International centre**, because **biometrics** are now captured at submission. **Postal applications are no longer accepted.**

## Who is eligible for an OCI card?

Broadly, you can apply if you are a foreign citizen (here, Canadian) and:

- you were a citizen of India at any time after 26 January 1950, **or**
- your parent, grandparent or great-grandparent was an Indian citizen, **or**
- you're the minor child of such a person, **or**
- you're the foreign spouse of an Indian citizen or OCI cardholder, with the marriage registered and subsisting for at least two years.

People who themselves, or whose parents or grandparents, were citizens of Pakistan or Bangladesh are not eligible. The exact wording is on the official OCI portal — check it if your family history is unusual.

## Step-by-step: applying for OCI from Canada

### 1. Fill the online form on the official OCI portal

Create the application on the government's OCI portal (linked below). Upload:

- a recent **photograph** in the required square format and size,
- your **signature** scan, in the dimensions the portal asks for.

These two uploads are the most common reason for rejection — keep a plain white background and follow the pixel and file-size limits exactly.

### 2. Gather your supporting documents

Typical documents for a **fresh OCI** for a former Indian citizen:

- Current **Canadian passport** (original + copies)
- Proof of **Indian origin** — your old Indian passport, or a parent's Indian passport/birth certificate
- **Surrender certificate** for your Indian passport, if you held Indian citizenship
- Proof of **address in your jurisdiction** (driver's licence, utility bill, etc.)
- For minors: birth certificate and both parents' documents
- For spouses: registered marriage certificate and the spouse's passport/OCI

The BLS checklist for your jurisdiction is the final word — print it and tick it off.

### 3. Book and attend your BLS appointment

Book a slot at the BLS centre for your jurisdiction and bring:

- the **printed online application**,
- **originals and copies** of every document,
- the **fees** (government fee + ICWF + BLS service fee + courier).

Your **biometrics** (fingerprints and photo) are taken at this visit, which is why everyone in the family who is applying must attend.

### 4. Track and collect

You can track the application online. BLS indicates a minimum of about **6–8 weeks** for the return of documents with the OCI booklet. The card (now a booklet) is returned by courier or collected, depending on what you chose.

## How much does OCI cost in Canada?

Fees are split between the Government of India and BLS. For the **Ottawa jurisdiction**, BLS currently lists:

| Service | Government fee | ICWF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh OCI | CAD 371 | CAD 5 |
| Re-issue after change of particulars | CAD 34 | CAD 5 |
| Duplicate (lost/damaged) | CAD 135 | CAD 5 |

On top of these there is a small **BLS service fee** and optional **courier** charges. Fees change, so always check the live fee page before paying.

## When do you need an OCI re-issue?

You need a re-issue if your **personal particulars change** — for example a name change after marriage or a change of nationality. Under current rules, the requirement to re-issue OCI every time you renew your foreign passport (for children and those over 50) was relaxed in recent years, but you should still **update your new passport details** on the OCI portal. If you're unsure which case you fall under, ask us before you apply.

## Common mistakes that delay OCI applications

- **Photo or signature not to spec** — the top reason for "query" emails.
- **Missing surrender certificate** for former Indian citizens.
- **Name mismatches** between documents (e.g. after marriage) without supporting proof.
- **Wrong jurisdiction** — always check by postal code first.
- **Assuming you can post it** — not since April 2026.
