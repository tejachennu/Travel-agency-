Bhutan feels like nowhere else. Prayer flags flutter on mountain passes, giant dzongs sit above rivers, and Tiger's Nest monastery clings to a cliff. A Bhutan trip from India is also unusually easy: no visa, a modest daily fee, and either a short flight into Paro or a scenic drive through Phuentsholing.

> **The short version:** Indians don't need a visa for Bhutan, but you do need a free **entry permit**. Get it on arrival at **Paro airport** or **Phuentsholing**, or online in advance, using a **passport or voter ID** (not Aadhaar). You also pay a **Sustainable Development Fee of ₹1,200 per person per night**, stay in government-certified hotels and travel with a licensed guide beyond Paro and Thimphu.

## Bhutan entry rules for Indians (2026)

Indian citizens don't need a visa. Instead, Bhutan's Department of Immigration issues an **entry permit**. The rules that matter:

- **Accepted ID for adults:** an Indian **passport valid for at least six months**, or an Election Commission **voter ID card**. **Aadhaar, PAN and driving licences are not accepted.**
- **Children under 18:** a passport, or a **birth certificate**, travelling with a parent.
- **Hotels:** you must stay in **Department of Tourism certified** accommodation for the whole trip.
- **Guide:** a **licensed Bhutanese guide** is needed for dzong and monastery visits and for travel beyond Paro and Thimphu, so it covers almost every real itinerary.
- **SDF:** paid per night when your permit is issued (details below).

Keep photocopies of every ID and two passport-size photos per person. Counters sometimes ask for them.

## The Bhutan SDF for Indians

Bhutan charges a **Sustainable Development Fee** to fund free healthcare, education and conservation, and to keep tourism "high value, low volume".

| Traveller | SDF per night (as of late 2026) |
| --- | --- |
| Indian adult (13+) | ₹1,200 / Nu 1,200 |
| Indian child aged 6 to 12 | Usually 50% (₹600) |
| Child under 6 | Exempt |
| Most other nationalities | USD 100 |

For a family of two adults and one 8-year-old on a 6-night trip, the SDF alone is roughly ₹18,000 (₹7,200 + ₹7,200 + ₹3,600). Budget it on top of hotels, transport and the guide. Bhutan has at times waived SDF for short stays in border towns such as Phuentsholing, but don't plan around it. Check the latest on the official site.

## Paro vs Phuentsholing: which entry is better?

### Flying into Paro

**Drukair** flies to Paro from **Delhi** and **Kolkata**, with some **Guwahati** and seasonal **Bagdogra** services, and **Bhutan Airlines** also serves India. Schedules change each season, so check the current timetable. The approach into Paro valley is famous for its views; sit on the left side flying in from Delhi for a chance of Himalayan peaks.

From Ahmedabad you connect via Delhi or Kolkata. Your **entry permit is issued at Paro airport immigration** after you show ID, hotel bookings and SDF payment (usually prepaid through your operator). Flights are limited and fill up in spring and autumn, so book early.

### Driving in through Phuentsholing

The budget route is to fly to **Bagdogra (Siliguri)** and drive about **160 km (4 to 5 hours)** through tea gardens to **Jaigaon–Phuentsholing**. Hasimara is the nearest railway station if you come by train.

At Phuentsholing you apply for the permit at the **Regional Immigration Office**, then pay the SDF beyond the Rinchending checkpoint just above town. The drive to **Thimphu** is about **150 to 170 km (5 to 6 hours)** on a winding mountain road. Most travellers overnight in Phuentsholing, so this route adds about **two days** to a trip.

### Applying online in advance

Indian travellers can apply for the permit online through Bhutan's immigration portal before arriving. Processing takes a few working days. It's worth doing in peak months, when Phuentsholing queues can be long. Licensed operators handle this along with the guide and hotels, which is how most of our travellers go.

## Permits beyond Thimphu and Paro

Your entry permit covers **Thimphu and Paro**. For **Punakha, Phobjikha, Bumthang, Haa** and other districts you need an extra **route permit**, issued by the immigration office in Thimphu (or arranged in advance by your operator). It's free but needs office hours, so a self-planned trip loses half a day in Thimphu. Your guide carries the permits and shows them at road checkpoints.

**Driving your own car from India** needs a separate vehicle permit and fees. With mountain roads and checkpoint paperwork, we don't recommend it for a first visit.

A common combination is to **fly into Paro and drive out through Phuentsholing to Bagdogra**. You see both sides of the country without backtracking.

### The Tiger's Nest hike

Taktsang sits at about **3,100 m**, roughly 900 m above the trailhead. Allow **5 to 6 hours return** at an easy pace, including time inside the monastery. Start by 8 am. Ponies go part of the way up (not down), and the café at the halfway viewpoint makes a good turnaround point for seniors or young kids.

Do the hike **at the end of the trip**, after a few days at altitude. Wear proper walking shoes, carry water, and know that bags and phones are left in lockers at the monastery.

## Best time to visit Bhutan

| Season | Months | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | March to May | Rhododendrons, mild days, Paro Tshechu festival (usually March or April) |
| Summer | June to August | Monsoon, green valleys, fewer tourists, landslide delays on roads |
| Autumn | September to November | Clearest skies, Thimphu Tshechu (usually September or October), peak demand |
| Winter | December to February | Cold, crisp and clear; Dochula views at their best; snow possible on passes |

Festival dates follow the lunar calendar, so check the year's dates with the Department of Tourism before fixing your trip. Diwali holidays from Gujarat fall right in Bhutan's best season, which is lovely but means booking hotels and Paro flights two to three months ahead.

## Where to stay

- **Paro:** valley-side hotels near town or farm stays out towards the Tiger's Nest trailhead.
- **Thimphu:** central hotels within walking distance of Norzin Lam and the craft bazaar are best for evenings out.
- **Punakha:** riverside and hillside resorts. Punakha is lower (about 1,200 m) and warmer than Thimphu.
- **Phuentsholing:** one night only, if entering by road.

Hotel category (3-star certified up to luxury lodges) is the biggest driver of cost after flights. The SDF is the same whichever hotel you choose.

## Vegetarian and Jain food in Bhutan

Bhutanese food is naturally vegetarian-friendly. Try **ema datshi** (chilli and cheese), **kewa datshi** (potato and cheese), **shamu datshi** (mushroom), vegetable momos and red rice. Be careful with ema datshi, because the chillies are the main ingredient, not a garnish.

Thimphu, Paro and Phuentsholing all have Indian restaurants, and certified hotels routinely serve dal, sabzi and roti. Jain meals (no onion, garlic or root vegetables) are possible if requested in advance, but choice is limited on long drive days. Carry theplas, khakhra and dry snacks. ## Money, SIM and practical tips

- **Currency:** the Bhutanese ngultrum (Nu) is pegged to the rupee and **Indian rupees are accepted** almost everywhere. Smaller notes are easiest, and you get change in ngultrum.
- **Payments:** some shops accept UPI, but coverage is patchy outside towns, so carry cash.
- **Phone:** Indian SIMs on roaming can be expensive. A local SIM from B-Mobile or TashiCell at Paro or Phuentsholing is cheap.
- **Time:** Bhutan is 30 minutes ahead of IST.
- **Dress code:** long sleeves and trousers or long skirts in dzongs and temples, no caps or hats inside, and no photos inside shrine rooms.
- **Smoking:** not allowed in public places. If you smoke, check the current rules on how much tobacco you can bring in.

## What to pack

Layers are key: warm jacket, fleece and thermals from November to March, light jacket even in summer. Pack comfortable walking shoes for Tiger's Nest, a cap and sunscreen (sun at altitude is strong), basic medicines including altitude and motion-sickness tablets for the mountain roads, and a rain jacket from June to September.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Carrying only Aadhaar.** You will be turned back. Bring a passport or voter ID.
- **Forgetting the kids' birth certificates.**
- **Booking non-certified homestays.** Your permit is tied to certified stays.
- **Planning Punakha without a route permit or guide.**
- **Doing Tiger's Nest on day one** before acclimatising.
- **Underestimating drive times.** Mountain roads average 30 to 40 km an hour.

## Who Bhutan suits

**Families** love the safety and slow pace. **Couples** come for Punakha's riverside resorts and the quiet. **Seniors** do very well if they fly into Paro and skip or shorten the Tiger's Nest hike. **Kids** over six usually manage the hike with breaks.
