Goa is India's favourite short escape: sun, sand and *susegad*, the Goan art of taking life easy. This Goa trip guide covers the questions our travellers from Gujarat ask most, from where to stay and which airport to use to how the Dudhsagar jeep safari works after the 2026 monsoon. It finishes with a relaxed 3N/4D plan.

> **The short version:** Stay in **North Goa** for energy or **South Goa** for calm (or split the trip), fly into **Mopa** for the north and **Dabolim** for the south, and go between **November and February**. As of mid-October 2026 the **Dudhsagar jeep safari has reopened** for the season, but only through authorised jeeps from Collem booked on the official portal. Railway-track treks remain banned.

## Best time to visit Goa

| Months | Weather | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| October | Showers taper off, green | Shacks reopening, Dudhsagar restarts, good deals |
| November – February | Sunny, dry, pleasant | Peak season; book early for Christmas and New Year |
| March – May | Hot and humid | Quieter beaches, lower rates |
| June – September | Monsoon | Lush and cheap; sea swimming and coastal water sports prohibited |

During the monsoon, Goa's lifeguard agency puts up red flags and advises everyone to stay out of the sea. That's a good time for food, churches, spice farms and lazy resort stays, not beach holidays.

## How to reach Goa from Ahmedabad

- **By air:** Goa has two airports. **Mopa (GOX, Manohar International)** is in the far north, about an hour from Calangute and Candolim. **Dabolim (GOI)** is near Vasco, convenient for Panaji's southern side and for South Goa (Colva is roughly 30–40 minutes away). Nonstop flights from Ahmedabad take about 1 hour 45 minutes, but there are only a few and schedules change by season. Many travellers connect via Mumbai. Choose the airport that matches where you're staying, because a Mopa–Palolem transfer can take well over two hours.
- **By train:** Konkan Railway trains from Gujarat stop at Madgaon (Margao) and Thivim. The journey takes the better part of a day, so it suits travellers with more time.

## North Goa or South Goa?

| | North Goa | South Goa |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vibe | Lively, shacks, nightlife | Calm, scenic, spacious |
| Beaches | Candolim, Calangute, Baga, Anjuna, Vagator, Morjim | Colva, Benaulim, Varca, Palolem, Agonda |
| Best for | Friends, first-timers, shoppers | Couples, families wanting quiet, seniors |
| Downside | Traffic and crowds in peak season | Fewer evening options, longer drives to sights |

### Where to stay in North Goa

- **Candolim and Sinquerim:** close to the action but calmer. This is our usual pick for families.
- **Calangute and Baga:** busiest and noisiest, with plenty to do on foot.
- **Anjuna and Vagator:** cafés, cliffs and a younger crowd.
- **Morjim, Ashwem and Mandrem:** wide, quiet beaches and closer to Mopa airport.
- **Panaji (Panjim):** a city base with the Fontainhas Latin quarter and river cruises.

### Where to stay in South Goa

- **Colva, Benaulim, Varca and Cavelossim:** big resorts with gardens and pools, easy with children.
- **Palolem, Patnem and Agonda:** beautiful bays with beach huts and boutique stays, best for couples.

## A 3N/4D Goa itinerary

### Day 1: Arrive and Baga sunset

Check in, rest, then head to **Candolim**, **Calangute** or **Baga** for a beach-shack sunset. Keep the first evening light, since you'll start early on Day 3.

### Day 2: North Goa forts and beaches

Start at **Fort Aguada** for the lighthouse and sea views, then move on to **Anjuna** and **Vagator** and climb **Chapora Fort** for the classic cliff view. In season, the **Anjuna flea market** runs on Wednesdays and the **Arpora Saturday night market** on Saturdays. In the evening, take a **Mandovi river cruise** from Panaji with music and Goan folk dances.

### Day 3: Dudhsagar, spice farm and Old Goa

Leave by 7 am. Collem, the starting point for the jeeps, is roughly 2–2.5 hours from the North Goa beaches. Do the **Dudhsagar jeep safari** in the morning (see below), have a **spice plantation lunch** near Ponda, and then visit the UNESCO-listed churches of **Old Goa** on the way back. It's a long day but the most memorable one.

### Day 4: Fontainhas or South Goa on the way out

Walk the colourful lanes of **Fontainhas** in Panaji. If you're flying out of Dabolim, stop at **Colva** or **Cabo de Rama** fort instead.

Prefer a slower trip? Add two nights in South Goa and you have an easy 5N/6D. See inclusions on our [Goa tour package](/destinations/goa).

## Dudhsagar Falls: 2026 status and how to visit

Dudhsagar ("sea of milk") drops about 310 metres down the Western Ghats on the Goa–Karnataka border, inside the Bhagwan Mahavir Wildlife Sanctuary. The falls are most powerful in and just after the monsoon.

### Is Dudhsagar open now?

**Yes, as of mid-October 2026.** In early October the Goa Forest Department reopened the Dudhsagar circuit for the 2026–27 tourist season. What's been reported so far:

- Entry is **only in Forest Department-registered jeeps** from **Collem (Kulem)**. You can't drive your own car to the falls.
- Bookings go through the **official GTDC portal** or authorised counters, and visitors are sent in hourly batches, roughly between **8 am and 3 pm**.
- There's a **daily cap on trips**, so weekends and holidays fill up.
- The Forest Department can **halt trips at short notice** if heavy rain returns.

Because these details are new for the season, we re-check the status the day before every Dudhsagar trip.

### Monsoon closure

The jeep safari is **suspended every monsoon**, typically from early or mid-June to early October. In 2025 it closed on 10 June. During the closure, the only legal way to see the falls is from a train on the Madgaon/Vasco–Londa line, which passes right in front of them.

### Can you trek to Dudhsagar?

- The famous **railway-track trek is not permitted**. Getting off trains at Dudhsagar station is banned, and the Railway Protection Force patrols the line and fines trespassers.
- **Independent forest treks are not allowed.** If an operator advertises a trek, ask to see its forest permission before you pay.

### Cost and practical tips

- In recent seasons, a jeep seat has cost roughly **₹500 per person**, plus small forest-entry and life-jacket charges. Check the current total on the official portal and **avoid touts**, who have charged several times the official rate.
- The jeep ride takes about 45 minutes each way through forest and river crossings. After that there's a short walk over rocks to the pool.
- **Life jackets** are compulsory if you enter the water, and you should swim only where staff allow it.
- Wear **sandals or shoes with grip**, carry a dry bag for phones, and avoid weekends if you can.
- It suits children who are comfortable on a bumpy ride. Seniors with back or knee problems may prefer to skip it.

## Old Goa churches

The **Basilica of Bom Jesus**, which holds the relics of St Francis Xavier, and the vast **Se Cathedral** across the road are part of the UNESCO World Heritage site *Churches and Convents of Goa*. Entry is free. Dress modestly, covering shoulders and knees. Photography inside the Basilica is generally restricted, and you should keep quiet during services. Allow about 1.5 hours, including the nearby Church of St Cajetan.

## Vegetarian and Jain food in Goa

Goa isn't only seafood. Goan Hindu homes have a rich vegetarian tradition: try **patal bhaji with pav**, **khatkhate** (mixed vegetable curry), **mushroom xacuti**, **alsande tonak** (bean curry) and a glass of **sol kadhi**. Panaji, Calangute and Margao have Gujarati, Rajasthani and pure-veg thali restaurants, and many hotels will cook Jain meals with notice. Beach shacks do offer vegetarian dishes, but they usually share a kitchen with fish and meat. Note that the famous dessert **bebinca contains eggs**.

## Getting around Goa

- **Car with driver:** the most relaxed option for families and for the Dudhsagar day.
- **Taxis:** use licensed tourist taxis or the state-backed app-based service. Mainstream ride-hailing apps are largely absent in Goa, so agree fares before you set off.
- **Scooters and self-drive cars:** rent only from licensed operators, carry your driving licence, and always wear a helmet. Goa's roads and night traffic catch out many first-timers.

## Rules worth knowing

- **Drinking alcohol in the open on beaches and cooking on the beach** can attract fines.
- **Sea swimming is prohibited during the monsoon.** At other times, swim only between the lifeguard flags.
- **Respect church dress codes** and ask before photographing people.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Booking Dudhsagar through a roadside tout.** Use the official portal or your travel planner.
- **Picking the wrong airport.** Mopa to South Goa, or Dabolim to Morjim, adds hours.
- **Leaving Christmas–New Year bookings late.** Beach-front rooms sell out months ahead.
- **Over-planning.** Goa rewards slow afternoons. Two big outings a day is plenty.
- **Underestimating the sun.** Carry sunscreen, a hat and water even in December.

## Packing list

- Sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat
- Light cotton clothes, plus one modest outfit for churches
- Sandals with grip for Dudhsagar and the forts
- A light rain jacket in October and from June to September
- Swimwear and a quick-dry towel

## Who this Goa plan suits

- **Friends' groups and first-timers:** base in Calangute–Baga or Anjuna.
- **Couples:** split North and South Goa, or stay in Palolem or Agonda.
- **Families and seniors:** Candolim or a South Goa resort, with a car and driver throughout.
