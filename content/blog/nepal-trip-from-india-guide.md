A Nepal trip from India is about as easy as international travel gets. There's no visa, Indian rupees are accepted, Hindi works almost everywhere, and you can be watching the Annapurnas from a Pokhara lakeside café a day after leaving Ahmedabad. The catch is the paperwork: the IDs that work at a land border are not the same as what airlines accept, and the rules on ₹500 notes changed in 2026.

> **The short version (October 2026):** Indians need **no visa** for Nepal. If you fly, carry a **valid passport or original voter ID card**; Aadhaar, PAN and driving licences are not accepted. From Ahmedabad, fly to **Kathmandu via Delhi or Mumbai**, or travel by train and road through **Sonauli** or **Raxaul**. Six days covers **Kathmandu, Pokhara and Chitwan**. You can now carry **₹200 and ₹500 notes up to ₹25,000 per person**.

## Documents for a Nepal trip from India

Indian citizens don't need a visa or a permit to visit Nepal for tourism. What matters is carrying the right photo ID, because airlines and immigration check it.

### Travelling by air

The Embassy of India in Kathmandu lists only **two valid documents for Indians flying between India and Nepal**:

- a **valid Indian passport**, or
- an **original voter ID card** with photograph issued by the Election Commission of India.

A downloaded or printed e-voter ID doesn't count. **Aadhaar, PAN card, driving licence and embassy registration certificates are not accepted.** Airline check-in staff in India follow this list, so you won't get past the counter with Aadhaar alone.

### Travelling by land

At land borders such as Sonauli and Raxaul, Indians walk or drive across without a visa or stamp. Even so, carry your passport or voter ID. You'll need it for hotel check-in, a Nepali SIM card, and any domestic flight inside Nepal.

### Children and senior citizens

- **Children:** minors can't hold a voter ID, so the simplest answer for a family that's flying is a **passport for each child**. Indian guidance has allowed children aged 15 to 18 to travel on an identity certificate from their school principal in a prescribed format, and some older circulars relaxed ID rules for children under 15 travelling with parents. Confirm with your airline before relying on either.
- **Seniors over 65:** older government guidance allowed other photo IDs (such as PAN, driving licence, CGHS card or ration card) for over-65s. The Embassy's current list for air travel doesn't include them, so seniors should carry a passport or voter ID too.

For anything unusual (an expired passport, a name mismatch), check the [Embassy of India in Kathmandu](https://www.indembkathmandu.gov.in/valid-travel-documents) before you book.

## Getting there from Ahmedabad

### By air (fastest)

Kathmandu's Tribhuvan International Airport is the main gateway. As of late 2026 there's no regular year-round nonstop from Ahmedabad, so most travellers connect via **Delhi or Mumbai**, which have direct flights to Kathmandu. Door to door, that's usually 6 to 9 hours depending on the layover. Seasonal or new routes do appear, so we check live schedules when we book.

Nepal also has newer international airports at **Pokhara** and **Bhairahawa** (Gautam Buddha International, near Lumbini). Their India links change often, so treat them as a bonus if a route lines up.

### By train and road (cheapest, slower)

Two land crossings make sense from Gujarat:

| Border crossing | Indian side | Reach it via | Onward in Nepal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sonauli – Bhairahawa** | Uttar Pradesh | Train to Gorakhpur, then about 95 km by road | Lumbini nearby; Pokhara or Chitwan by road |
| **Raxaul – Birgunj** | Bihar | Train to Raxaul, often with a change | Chitwan in roughly 3–4 hours; Kathmandu by road |

The train from Ahmedabad to Gorakhpur alone takes well over a day, so this route suits travellers with time and a smaller budget, or those adding Lumbini. If you're driving an Indian-registered car, you'll need a vehicle customs permit (*bhansar*) at the border.

## A 6-day Kathmandu, Pokhara and Chitwan itinerary

This loop is our standard plan for a first Nepal trip from India. It works for families and seniors because no day has more than one long drive.

| Day | Base | Highlights | Travel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Kathmandu | Arrive, Pashupatinath evening aarti | Airport transfer |
| 2 | Kathmandu | Swayambhunath, Patan or Bhaktapur, Boudhanath | City drives |
| 3 | Pokhara | Manakamana cable car en route, Phewa Lake | About 200 km, 6–8 hrs by road (or ~25 min flight) |
| 4 | Pokhara | Sarangkot sunrise, Peace Pagoda, Davis Falls | Local |
| 5 | Chitwan | Tharu village, Rapti riverbank sunset | About 4–5 hrs by road |
| 6 | Kathmandu | Morning canoe or jeep safari, fly home | 5–6 hrs road or ~20 min flight from Bharatpur |

### Day 1: Arrive in Kathmandu

Check in around **Thamel** (lively, plenty of restaurants) or **Durbar Marg / Lazimpat** (quieter, better for families). In the evening, go to **Pashupatinath Temple** for the aarti on the Bagmati ghats. Hindus can enter the main temple; others can watch from across the river.

### Day 2: Kathmandu valley heritage

Start early at **Swayambhunath** (the "monkey temple") for views over the valley. Then choose one of the old royal squares: **Patan** for temples and metalwork shops, or **Bhaktapur** for pottery squares and its famous *juju dhau* (king curd). End at **Boudhanath**, one of the largest stupas in the world, when the butter lamps are lit. Fit travellers can book an early-morning **Everest mountain flight** before sightseeing.

### Day 3: Drive to Pokhara

The Prithvi Highway follows the Trishuli river. Break the drive at Kurintar for the **Manakamana cable car**, which goes up to a much-loved Bhagwati temple; it's a highlight for many of our Gujarati travellers. Reach Pokhara by late afternoon and take a boat on **Phewa Lake** to the island **Tal Barahi temple**. If road time is a concern, fly instead: Kathmandu–Pokhara is about 25 minutes.

### Day 4: Pokhara

Drive up to **Sarangkot** for sunrise over Annapurna, Machhapuchhre and Dhaulagiri (October to April gives the best odds). After breakfast, visit **Bindhyabasini temple**, **Davis Falls**, **Gupteshwor Mahadev cave** and the **World Peace Pagoda**. Adventure options include paragliding from Sarangkot and ultralight flights. Stay at **Lakeside** for easy walking and restaurants.

### Day 5: Chitwan National Park

Drive about 4–5 hours to **Sauraha**, the village at the edge of Chitwan National Park. In the afternoon visit a **Tharu village** and watch sunset from the Rapti riverbank. Many lodges include an evening Tharu cultural dance.

### Day 6: Safari and fly home

Take an early **canoe ride** on the Rapti (look for gharials and birds) or a **jeep safari** for a chance of one-horned rhinos. Park entry for SAARC nationals is NPR 1,000 per person, as listed by the park. Then fly from **Bharatpur to Kathmandu** (about 20 minutes) for your evening flight to India, or drive back. If you came in by land, Chitwan is also a sensible exit towards Birgunj or Sonauli.

## Indian currency rules in Nepal

The Nepali rupee is pegged to the Indian rupee at a fixed **₹1 = NPR 1.60**. Indian rupees are widely accepted in Kathmandu, Pokhara and border towns, but change is usually given in Nepali rupees.

- **₹100 notes and smaller:** can be carried and used without a cap.
- **₹200 and ₹500 notes:** after years of restrictions, India amended its currency rules in late 2025, and **Nepal Rastra Bank confirmed in July 2026** that Indian and Nepali nationals may carry ₹200 and ₹500 notes **up to a combined ₹25,000 per person**. Only notes issued after the November 2016 demonetisation are valid.
- **₹2,000 notes:** still **not allowed** in Nepal.

Some small shops and taxis may still hesitate over ₹500 notes, since the change is recent. Carry a stack of ₹100 notes for tips, temples and taxis. UPI payments to Nepali merchants enrolled with the Fonepay QR network have been available since 2024, but acceptance varies, so don't rely on UPI alone. Cards work in bigger hotels and restaurants.

## Best time to visit Nepal

| Months | Season | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| October – November | Autumn | Best mountain views, pleasant days; Dashain and Tihar festivals make it busy |
| December – February | Winter | Clear skies, cold mornings and nights in Kathmandu and Pokhara; good value |
| March – May | Spring | Rhododendrons, warm days; haze can hide peaks; Chitwan gets hot by May |
| June – September | Monsoon | Green but cloudy, landslide risk on highways, flight delays |

For a first trip from Gujarat, **October–November** and **February–April** are the sweet spots. Diwali (Tihar in Nepal) is lovely to experience but book well ahead.

## What a Nepal trip costs

Nepal is one of the better-value international trips from India, but a few things drive the final price:

- **Flights:** the Ahmedabad–Kathmandu return fare is usually the biggest single cost and climbs sharply around Diwali and school holidays. The land route cuts this to train fares plus road transfers.
- **Hotels:** Kathmandu and Pokhara have everything from simple guesthouses to international chains. Lakeside Pokhara and Thamel are the best-value areas.
- **Vehicle and driver:** a private car for the Kathmandu–Pokhara–Chitwan loop is the most comfortable option for families and seniors, and costs more than tourist buses.
- **Domestic flights:** they save hours of driving but add noticeably to the budget, and fares for non-Nepalis are higher than local fares.
- **Activities:** safari, cable car, mountain flight and paragliding are all extras.

## Food: vegetarian and Jain travellers

Nepal is easy for vegetarians. **Dal bhat** (rice, lentils, vegetables and pickle) is the national meal, and **Thakali thalis** in Pokhara are excellent. Veg momos are everywhere. Kathmandu and Pokhara have many Indian and Marwari bhojanalayas serving Gujarati-style food. For Jain meals, ask for "no onion, no garlic" when ordering; most hotels will manage if you tell them in advance. Carry some theplas or khakhras for the long drives.

## Practical tips and mistakes to avoid

- **Don't rely on Aadhaar.** It's the most common reason Indian travellers are stopped at check-in.
- **Nepal is 15 minutes ahead of India** (IST + 0:15), so adjust alarms for safari pick-ups.
- **Keep a buffer day** in monsoon or if you're flying domestically; small-aircraft flights are often delayed by weather.
- **Roaming is expensive.** Buy a local SIM (Ncell or Nepal Telecom) with your passport or voter ID.
- **Pack layers.** Even in October, Sarangkot at sunrise and Kathmandu nights are chilly; Chitwan stays warm.
- **Respect temple rules.** Remove shoes, walk clockwise around stupas and ask before photographing rituals at Pashupatinath.
