Waking up above a turquoise lagoon, snorkelling with turtles before breakfast and watching the sunset from your own deck: a Maldives honeymoon from India is far easier to pull off than most couples think. It is a short flight away, there is no visa to apply for, and with the right resort, villa and meal plan you can get the water-villa experience without paying top-tier prices for every night.

> **The short version:** Indians get a **free 30-day visa on arrival** in the Maldives. File the free **IMUGA Traveller Declaration** within 96 hours before landing, carry your resort booking and return ticket, and plan **4 nights** between **December and April** for the calmest seas. Choose a **speedboat resort** for convenience or a **seaplane resort** for seclusion, and pick **half board or all-inclusive**, because you can't eat off the island.

## Maldives visa for Indians and IMUGA

You don't apply for anything in advance. At Velana International Airport in Malé, Indian passport holders receive a **free 30-day tourist visa on arrival**, as long as you can show:

- a **passport with a machine-readable zone**. Maldives Immigration officially asks for at least one month of validity, but we recommend six months because airlines and transit countries often check for it. Passports with handwritten or manually extended validity are not accepted.
- a **confirmed, prepaid booking** at a registered resort, hotel or guesthouse,
- a **confirmed return or onward ticket**,
- **enough funds** for your stay (officers rarely ask on a resort booking, but keep a card and some USD handy).

### The IMUGA Traveller Declaration

Every traveller, including children, must submit the **Traveller Declaration on the official IMUGA portal** (imuga.immigration.gov.mv). It is **free** and can be filed **up to 96 hours before arrival**. You'll need passport details, flight numbers and your resort name. Save the QR code on your phone. Ignore websites that charge a "processing fee": they aren't official. The departure declaration was dropped in 2024, so you only file it for arrival.

### What not to pack

Maldivian customs prohibits bringing in **alcohol, pork products, idols for worship and vapes or e-cigarettes**, and they are confiscated on arrival. If you travel with a small idol or photo frame for daily puja, leave it at home or ask us first. Alcohol is freely available at resorts, not on local islands.

## Getting there from Ahmedabad

There's **no regular non-stop flight from Ahmedabad to Malé** as of late 2026. Most couples from Gujarat connect through:

- **Mumbai**: the most popular route, with daily non-stops to Malé.
- **Bengaluru**: a short hop of about two hours to Malé.
- **Delhi**: direct flights of roughly four to five hours.

Allow most of a day door to door with the connection. Schedules change by season, so we check current timings before suggesting a resort. That matters because of how resort transfers work.

## How Maldives resorts work

Each resort occupies its own island. You land in Malé and the resort's team meets you in the arrivals hall. From there it's one of three transfers:

| Transfer | Typical time from Malé | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speedboat | 15 to 60 minutes | Late arrivals, shorter trips, tighter budgets | Choppy rides in rough weather |
| Seaplane | Roughly 25 to 60 minutes | Remote atolls, clearer water, that aerial view | Daylight only, strict baggage limits, higher cost |
| Domestic flight + boat | 1 to 2 hours in total | Far-flung atolls such as Laamu or Addu | Two legs, so more connection risk |

**The seaplane trap:** seaplanes only fly between roughly sunrise and late afternoon. If your flight lands in Malé in the evening, you'll need a night in Malé or Hulhumalé before flying out, which means a wasted honeymoon night. Seaplanes also have a lower baggage allowance (often around 20 kg checked plus a small hand bag per person), and excess is charged per kilo.

### Which atoll suits you?

- **North and South Malé Atolls:** speedboat access, the widest choice of resorts and the easiest logistics. Ideal for a 3 to 4 night trip.
- **Ari Atoll (North and South):** seaplane access, excellent snorkelling and diving, and whale sharks in South Ari all year round.
- **Baa Atoll:** a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. Hanifaru Bay draws large groups of manta rays, usually between June and November.
- **Lhaviyani, Raa and Noonu:** quieter, more upmarket resorts, reached by seaplane or a domestic flight plus boat.

## Beach villa or water villa?

A **water villa** is the iconic image, with steps straight into the lagoon, a deck over the water and sometimes glass floor panels. A **beach villa** sits on the sand under palms, is usually cheaper and is often more private, with the beach right outside your door.

Our favourite honeymoon trick is to **split the stay: 2 nights in a beach villa, then 2 nights in a water villa**. You get both experiences and end the trip on a high. A few more choices to think about:

- **Sunset vs sunrise side:** sunset-facing villas cost more. Sunrise villas are quieter and cheaper.
- **Private pool:** lovely, but in a water villa the whole lagoon is your pool. Pay for it on a beach villa if privacy matters.
- **Adults-only resorts:** worth considering if you want a calm, couples-focused stay.
- **Distance from the jetty:** the last villas on a long walkway are the most private but need a buggy or a walk to reach the restaurants.

## Picking the right meal plan

You can't leave a resort island to eat elsewhere, and a la carte prices are high, so the meal plan shapes your final bill more than almost anything else.

| Plan | Includes | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bed & breakfast | Breakfast only | Very short stays or light eaters |
| Half board | Breakfast + dinner | Most couples who enjoy a late, big breakfast |
| Full board | All three meals | Relaxed stays with few excursions |
| All-inclusive | Meals, selected drinks, snacks, often some activities | Couples who want no bills at checkout |

**Vegetarian and Jain food:** Indian travellers are a big market for the Maldives, so most resorts have Indian dishes on the menu and many have Indian chefs. Pure vegetarian meals are easy. For **Jain meals** (no onion, garlic or root vegetables), tell the resort in writing at the time of booking and remind them on arrival. If you don't drink, all-inclusive may not be worth the premium; half board plus a few paid dinners often works out cheaper.

## Best time for a Maldives honeymoon

The Maldives is warm all year (around 27 to 31°C). What changes is rain, wind and sea conditions.

| Months | Weather | Prices | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| December to March | Dry, sunny, calm seas | Highest, especially over Christmas and New Year | Peak wedding-season honeymoons; book early |
| April | Hot, mostly dry | High, easing | Great visibility for snorkelling |
| May | Transition, showers begin | Falling | Good value shoulder month |
| June to September | Wetter, windier, short heavy showers | Lowest | Manta season in Baa Atoll; surf season |
| October to November | Mixed, drying out | Moderate | November is a strong value pick |

For Gujarati weddings in November to February, the dry season lines up perfectly. If you're marrying in summer, don't rule out June to September: it rarely rains all day, and resort offers are often generous.

## What drives the cost of a Maldives honeymoon

We don't quote fixed prices because they swing so much with season and resort category, but these are the factors that move the number:

- **Season:** peak December to March can cost far more than the same villa in June.
- **Transfer type:** a return seaplane transfer is often several hundred US dollars per person, while speedboats near Malé cost much less.
- **Villa category:** water villas and sunset-facing villas carry a clear premium.
- **Meal plan:** all-inclusive can add a lot per night but removes the surprise bill.
- **Taxes and charges:** resort rates are often quoted before a **service charge of around 10%**, **Tourism GST of 17%** (raised from 16% in July 2025) and a **green tax of USD 12 per person per night**. Always ask whether a quote is "all-in".
- **Flights:** connecting fares from Ahmedabad rise sharply in the wedding season and around Diwali.

**Ways to save:** travel in November, April or May; choose a speedboat resort; split beach and water villas; book a resort that runs honeymoon offers (many add a cake, a dinner or a spa credit with a marriage certificate dated within the last few months).

## Things to do as a couple

- **Snorkel the house reef:** many resorts have reef sharks, turtles and rays a few metres from the beach. Snorkel gear is often free.
- **Sandbank picnic:** a boat drops you on a strip of sand in the middle of the ocean.
- **Sunset dolphin cruise:** spinner dolphins are a common sight.
- **Underwater dining:** a handful of resorts have restaurants below sea level; book these early.
- **Spa over the water:** most resorts offer couples' treatments.
- **Malé city stop:** if you have a few hours before your flight, see the old Friday Mosque area and the fish market.

## Packing checklist

- **Reef-safe sunscreen**, a rash guard and a hat; the sun is intense.
- **A waterproof phone pouch** and a dry bag for boat trips.
- **Light, modest clothes** for Malé, the airport and any local-island trip; swimwear is fine at resorts.
- **USD in small notes** for tips; cards work everywhere in resorts.
- **Medicines** for seasickness if you are taking a speedboat.
- **Your marriage certificate** (a copy) for honeymoon offers.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Booking a seaplane resort with an evening arrival** and losing a night in Malé.
- **Comparing quotes that aren't like for like**: one may include taxes and transfers, another may not.
- **Leaving the meal plan as B&B** and paying resort prices for every lunch and dinner.
- **Forgetting IMUGA** or filling it on a paid look-alike site.
- **Packing an idol or alcohol** in your bag.
